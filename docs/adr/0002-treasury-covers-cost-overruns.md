# The treasury covers cost overruns, with no cap

A Listing's Funding target is calculated from its planned costs, and escrow can never be overdrawn, so a real cost above the plan (a price rise, demurrage) needs new money. Finance (or the owner) may first allow Sale proceeds already received on an earlier Shipment to pay it. Otherwise the treasury puts in an Overrun fill: ops requests it with proof, finance or the owner approves it, and there is no cap. It is a normal Contribution that shares profit and loss pro-rata. We chose this so a trade is never stranded halfway through, even though it moves the risk onto the platform and dilutes everyone else's slice.

## Considered options

- **The Manager pays the overrun as a cost** that earns nothing and is repaid only if the result covers it. Rejected, although it keeps the Manager accountable for their own plan.
- **The Manager adds capital** as an extra Contribution. Rejected.
- **A cap** (for example 10% of the Funding target, with the owner above it). Rejected: finance approves every fill, whatever the size.

## Consequences

- A Manager who under-plans costs loses nothing extra. Investors' slice of the pool shrinks, and the treasury carries the gap. The reviewer's check of planned costs against the market is the main safeguard.
- On a Shareholder Listing, an Overrun fill is a treasury Contribution, so its profit goes into the Shareholder share split (ADR 0001).
