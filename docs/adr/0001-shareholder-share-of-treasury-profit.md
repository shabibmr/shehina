# Shareholders receive most of the treasury's profit on their own trades

About ten importers each hold 1% of Shehnat, and we want their suppliers to trade through the platform. On a Listing where a Shareholder is the buyer or the manager, 75% of the profit on every treasury contribution goes to that Shareholder's wallet at settlement, and 25% goes to a separate Reserve fund. When the buyer is a Shareholder, owner or finance may also waive the manager deposit, and the treasury stands in for it (20% of the target). This pays Shareholders for bringing in trade, per Listing, instead of through company dividends, and it keeps the deal on the platform's ledger rather than in side agreements.

## Considered options

- **Treasury replaces the whole target**, so the Listing is never offered to investors. Rejected: investors still fund the rest of the target.
- **The Shareholder bears 75% of the treasury's loss** to mirror the profit split. Rejected: the treasury bears its own loss, and the Shareholder takes none.
- **Share the 75% across all Shareholders.** Rejected: it goes only to the Shareholder(s) on the Listing, split equally if the manager and the buyer are different Shareholders.

## Consequences

- ~~The Shareholder relationship is not shown to investors.~~ Amended by ADR 0004: investors are told that a Listing is a Shareholder Listing, but not which Shareholder. A Shareholder-buyer's price affects their returns. Reviewers check the expected sale price against the market as a safeguard, with no fixed tolerance yet.
- A Shariah board may treat the payment as a hidden discount to the buyer. This is unresolved.
- A Shareholder may be both the manager and the buyer on one Listing (self-trade) and receive the whole 75%.
