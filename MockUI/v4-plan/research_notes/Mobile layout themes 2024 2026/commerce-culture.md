# Commerce and culture-retail mobile layouts, 2024–2026-10-31

Scope note: evidence was taken from maker copy, store listings, official help, one vendor case study, and a small set of secondary teardowns. Screenshot images on store listings were present but not visually described by the fetch, so pixel-level claims (exact columns, corner radius, type size) are included only where a source states them. Pre-2023 material is labeled historical and is not treated as a current layout. No product, quote, or screenshot was invented. Research cutoff is 2026-10-31.

## Which live shopping or culture-retail products show a distinctive structural layout?

### Takeaway
Nine products have a structural move that is more than a decorated card stack, with very different levels of proof. The strongest are Aesop’s live storefront (type-and-hairline catalog, not a confirmed native app), StockX and GOAT (the product page is a market, not a buy card), Nike SNKRS (the home is a launch event), Depop Outfits (a freeform collage beside the shop), and Shopify’s Shop app (the home became a swipeable merchant-branded feed). Zara, SSENSE, Mr Porter, the Apple Store app, and TOTEME are only partly verified. COS, Glossier, Reformation, Farfetch, and Arc’teryx did not qualify on the sources opened.

### Cited Findings
- Aesop’s public storefront at aesop.com, captured into an unofficial DESIGN.md by Dov Azencot and updated 13 May 2026, is a cream-paper catalog: 0px corners on buttons, inputs, tiles, and cards; 1px graphite hairlines instead of card shadows; product photography carries the warmth. The same extraction says the product rail is 3-up on desktop, 2-up on tablet, and 1-up on mobile. No native Aesop shopping app was found. — [shadcn.io Aesop DESIGN.md](https://www.shadcn.io/design/aesop)
- StockX’s own help says the Verified Marketplace product page offers two purchases: Make an Offer at a chosen price, or Buy Now at the current lowest Ask. A separate Listings Marketplace (used or unique items) is on StockX.com and the US iOS app. A Live Marketplace is on the StockX iOS app for US users only. — [How do I buy on StockX?](https://stockx.com/help/articles/how-do-i-buy-on-stockx); [Make Offer vs Buy Now](https://stockx.com/help/articles/What-is-the-difference-between-Make-Offer-and-Buy-Now-when-purchasing)
- GOAT’s live app, in a ScreensDesign flow teardown, puts instant buy and a lower offer on the same product page, and has a separate Inspiration tab that shops items out of styled photos. Capture date of that video is not stated. — [ScreensDesign, GOAT](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/goat-sneakers-apparel)
- Nike’s current SNKRS store listing (updated 23 Oct 2026) describes the product as launches, not a stable catalog: upcoming drops, SNKRS Pass reserve-and-pickup, invite-only access, and “unique launch scavenger hunts” whose access “might be hidden in geo-located events, scannable augmented reality targets, or scratchable images,” plus SNKRS Stories and SNKRS Maps. — [Nike SNKRS on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nike.snkrs&hl=en_US)
- Depop shipped Outfits on 24 Sep 2025: a mood-board canvas opened from a scissors icon on a listing, with color backgrounds, resize and placement, layout templates, and shoppable items. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/depop-launches-a-fashion-collaging-tool-to-style-pinterest-worthy-outfits/)
- Shopify’s changelog of 11 Dec 2024 says the Shop app home feed was redesigned around merchant imagery, video, and brand colors. A 9 Feb 2026 Shopifreaks report, quoting a Harley Finkelstein LinkedIn post that this research did not open directly, says the home was redesigned again as a swipeable feed with shoppable video in front. — [Shopify Changelog](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/shop-app-has-a-new-merchant-focused-home-feed); [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/shopify-redesigns-the-shop-app-home-feed-for-a-video-centric-swipeable-shopping-experience/)
- Zara’s app (Inditex) was updated 28 Oct 2026. The Play “What’s new” line says the app was redesigned. A third-party screenshot commentary describes campaign imagery on home, a minimal persistent bottom bar, and a product page that shows a price (example given: ABRIGO LARGO CRUZADO at 79,95 EUR). — [Zara on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.inditex.zara&hl=en_US); [MWM Zara](https://mwm.ai/apps/zara/547951480)
- SSENSE’s Play listing, still shipping updates into 2026, describes an infinite scroll, a home built from browsed-brand arrivals, recently viewed, wishlist, and category spotlights, and a product page with full-look styling plus a scroll of similar items. — [SSENSE on Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ssense.android.prod&hl=en_US)
- Mr Porter’s own app page (en-mx, returned in search; a later fetch of the US URL was access-denied) says the app is for latest arrivals and “up-to-the-moment style advice from The Journal,” with new Journal features every day and new arrivals three times a week. — [MR PORTER app page](https://www.mrporter.com/en-mx/content/app/)
- Apple’s App Store listing and July 2024 release notes replace Shop and Sessions with Products and Go Further, beside For You. Reporting in Nov 2025 and Jan 2025 still lists For You, Products, Go Further, Bag, and Search. The Nov 2025 change that was actually described is Liquid Glass on the bar, icon, buttons, and menus — a material change, not a new layout. — [Apple Store on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/apple-store/id375380948/); [MacRumors, 16 Jul 2024](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/07/16/apple-store-app-overhaul/); [9to5Mac, 13 Nov 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/13/apple-store-app-updated-liquid-glass/); [Forbes, 17 Jan 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/prakharkhanna/2025/01/17/apple-strengthens-india-retail-presence-with-apple-store-app-launch/)
- TOTEME’s app, built with Venn Apps, is described by the vendor as full-bleed imagery and uncluttered layouts, plus a dedicated Shop The Look page and a members section called ℅ TOTEME. Launch year is not stated. — [Venn Apps, TOTEME](https://www.vennapps.com/case-studies/toteme)
- COS: no official COS fashion app was found. The COS site does publish numbered runway looks (“LOOK 01” onward) and a “shop the collection” rail. That is a website editorial, not a verified phone layout. — [COS runway collection](https://www.cos.com/en-no/features/collections/the-womens-aw25-runway-collection)
- Arc’teryx’s app page lists notifications, a personalized explore feed, compare, and product advice. It does not describe a layout that is structurally different from a standard shop. — [Arc'teryx app](https://arcteryx.com/us/en/arcteryx-app)
- Glossier: the opened hits are a 2020 mobile-web UX essay and later student or Dribbble concepts, not a shipped 2024–2026 app. — [UX Collective, 3 Mar 2020](https://uxdesign.cc/ux-review-7-delightful-experiences-on-glossiers-mobile-app-6784ef8a3ffd)
- Reformation and Farfetch: no 2024–2026 primary page describing a distinctive mobile layout was found in this pass.

### Inferences
- The layouts that survive contact with a primary source are structural roles for the screen: a market (two prices), a launch event, a collage canvas, a type-led index, or a swipe of merchant films. “Clean premium” and Liquid Glass do not qualify by themselves.
- A culture-retail app can be distinctive in only one of its screens (Depop Outfits, TOTEME Shop The Look) while the rest of the shop stays ordinary. That still counts, because the structure changes, not just the skin.

### Gaps
- Store screenshots for Zara, SNKRS, and SSENSE were not viewable as images here, so column count, type size, and corner language for those apps are unverified.
- COS, Glossier, Reformation, Farfetch, and Arc’teryx were not shown to have a distinctive shipped mobile layout in 2024–2026. Absence of a page is not proof they lack one.
- ScreensDesign’s GOAT capture date is unknown, so it is not pinned to a year inside the window.

## What is the one structural move, and what stays fixed?

### Takeaway
Each qualifying product keeps one chrome or one transaction rule fixed, and lets a single composition change: the photograph, the price, the launch mechanic, or the arrangement of the same goods.

### Cited Findings
- Aesop. Fixed: cream canvas `#fffef2`, graphite ink `#333333` used as both text and 1px rule, 0px radius on commerce chrome, Suisse Intl (or SuisseIntl-Medium) for almost all UI. Changes: photography, and exactly one Zapf-Humanist serif at 31px for the “New and notable” headline. The extraction says there is no `box-shadow` on product chrome; the four shadows it counted sit on a motion player. Hero band is described as a 50/50 split of full-bleed image and an overlay copy card. On mobile the product rail becomes 1-up. — [Aesop DESIGN.md](https://www.shadcn.io/design/aesop)
- StockX. Fixed: anonymous bid/ask matching. On the product page the shopper either Buys Now at the lowest Ask or Makes an Offer. The about page draws this as one price stage (“Lowest Ask”, then Buy Now or Make Offer). What changes is the number, and which marketplace the item sits in (Verified, Listings, or Live). StockX Pro’s Market Data panel, a seller tool, adds a sales graph filterable by date and a Market Comparison of the bid/ask spread, including the price required to become the lowest Ask. — [how to buy](https://stockx.com/help/articles/how-do-i-buy-on-stockx); [about buying](https://stockx.com/about/buying/); [Pro market data, 29 Sep 2025](https://stockx.com/news/how-to-view-market-data-on-stockx-pro/)
- GOAT. Fixed: product photography on a black-and-white UI, with a sell action on the product page. Changes: the shopper picks Buy at the listed price or switches into an offer, and the offer screen adds Top Offer and Last Sale. A separate Inspiration tab recomposes the same goods as shoppable styled photos. ScreensDesign also notes a circular slider for how long an offer lasts. At one moment in the recording, Add to Cart is disabled because the price “fluctuates rapidly,” and the path is forced through Checkout. — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/goat-sneakers-apparel)
- SNKRS. Fixed: a Nike account with name, size, and shipping saved so launch day can complete. Changes: the access mechanic. Nike’s own text lists ordinary shop, SNKRS Pass (reserve, then pick up in a shoe store), invite-only launches, and surprise access hidden in a geo event, an AR target, or a scratchable image. Stories and Maps are further surfaces, not another catalog grid. Bottom-tab names were not on the Play listing that was opened. A third-party Android review that names Home, Discover, orders, and profile is not treated as current. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nike.snkrs&hl=en_US)
- Depop Outfits. Fixed: the listing. The new structure is a canvas: scissors icon on the right of a listing, then background color, size, placement, and templates. Items in the collage stay shoppable; a sold-out item is replaced by similar suggestions. Static images can be shared to Pinterest and Instagram. The underlying shop grid’s column count was not opened. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/depop-launches-a-fashion-collaging-tool-to-style-pinterest-worthy-outfits/)
- Shop. Fixed: Shopify’s own templates, not the merchant’s theme (this last point is from a designer guide seen only as a search extract and is unverified here). What the changelog confirms: the home feed now carries each merchant’s imagery, video, and brand colors. What Shopifreaks reports for Feb 2026: a vertical swipe, with product-shot video or a cropped cover video sitting behind a product carousel. — [Changelog, 11 Dec 2024](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/shop-app-has-a-new-merchant-focused-home-feed); [Shopifreaks, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.shopifreaks.com/shopify-redesigns-the-shop-app-home-feed-for-a-video-centric-swipeable-shopping-experience/)
- Zara. Fixed, per MWM’s captions of current screenshots: a minimal bottom navigation that stays present on the campaign home and on collection screens. What changes: home is fashion-campaign imagery (their captions name an Autumn Winter 2025 campaign and a Kids studio collection in search extracts; the re-fetched page captions say “striking fashion campaign imagery” and a Kids collection without repeating the season). The product page is a garment photograph with a price. Play reviews indexed in search, one dated 18 Jul 2026, say more photos are reached by swiping down, and a horizontal swipe jumps to the next product. Those review texts were not in the default review sample on a later full fetch of the same Play URL, so they are search-indexed reviews, not a re-read quote. — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/zara/547951480); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.inditex.zara&hl=en_US)
- SSENSE. Fixed: a text-led product fact stack. On a live PDP the order is brand, product name, material bullets, price, add to bag, wishlist, then “Styled with.” The Play listing says the home is not one grid: it is modules for browsed brands, recently viewed, wishlist, and category spotlights, over an infinite scroll. What changes is which module is filled. Visual grid density is not verified. A 2021 App Store review that says three products per row is historical only. — [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ssense.android.prod&hl=en_US); [live PDP text](https://www.ssense.com/en-gb/women/product/weekend-max-mara/white-dagile-t-shirt/18800741)
- Mr Porter. Fixed: shopping. The second structure is The Journal, read in the same app and shoppable from the story. New arrivals are scheduled (Monday, Wednesday, Friday on the app page’s feature list in the search extract). Screen geometry was not opened. — [app page](https://www.mrporter.com/en-mx/content/app/)
- Apple Store. Fixed: bottom tabs For You, Products, Go Further, Bag, and Search (Forbes, Jan 2025; 9to5Mac, Nov 2025). What changes is the job of the tab: personal recommendations and saved items, the hardware catalog, or post-purchase setup and Today at Apple. A pinned buy bar and a full-bleed product stage were not described in any source opened. Do not assume them. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/apple-store/id375380948/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/prakharkhanna/2025/01/17/apple-strengthens-india-retail-presence-with-apple-store-app-launch/)
- TOTEME. Fixed: the site’s full-bleed imagery, type, and white space, carried into the app. Two added structures, both vendor-described: a Shop The Look page where a whole outfit is added in two taps, and ℅ TOTEME, a members concierge (styling appointments, style guides, care, nearest atelier, “Ask the Concierge”) segmented by loyalty tier rather than points. — [Venn Apps](https://www.vennapps.com/case-studies/toteme)

### Inferences
- “What is fixed” is the brand’s contract with the thumb: Aesop’s paper rules, StockX’s two verbs, Zara’s bottom bar, SNKRS’ saved size and address. “What changes” is the composition of goods, not a new visual theme per screen.
- The Apple Store app’s 2024 move is informational architecture (three peer modes plus a bag), not a product stage. It belongs in this set only for navigation structure.

### Gaps
- Whether SSENSE’s duplicated price-and-add-to-bag block in crawled HTML is a sticky buy bar, or the crawler seeing two breakpoints, was not resolved.
- Zara’s vertical-photo / horizontal-next-product gesture is attested by search-indexed Play reviews, including one dated 18 Jul 2026, and by a Dec 2018 review that already complained about vertical swapping. It may be a long-standing gesture, not a 2024–2026 invention. It was not confirmed on a screenshot.
- SNKRS’ feed, countdown, dark ground, and 3D viewer appear in a NextSprints teardown and were not corroborated by Nike’s listing. They are unverified and should not be used.
- No source opened here described safe-area insets or the exact height of a bottom buy bar for any of these apps, except Aesop’s 48px primary button and 51px top nav, which are from a desktop-leaning extraction.

## What style system is actually visible?

### Takeaway
Only Aesop has a style system specific enough to copy without inventing: one warm paper, one graphite, almost no accent, square corners, hairline instead of shadow, and type that stays at weight 400. Everyone else in this set is documented as a layout, not as a token system. Liquid Glass, gradients, and neon were not the distinguishing move in any source that qualified.

### Cited Findings
- Aesop base and surface: canvas `#fffef2` (warm paper, not pure white); warm taupe `#f7ecdd` behind some heroes; cool gray `#e4e9ea` only on motion-player chrome. Ink `#333333` is the text and the hairline. Pure black `#000000` is reserved for some icon strokes. The extractor counted graphite 2101 times and terracotta `#945c26` in 10 borders, used on gift-set tiles, not as a button fill. Accent amber `#e9962d` is described as a rare rating highlight. — [Aesop DESIGN.md](https://www.shadcn.io/design/aesop)
- Aesop type roles: Suisse Intl at 14px / 1.5 for running text; captions and prices at 11px / 1.2; nav and buttons at 14px; one 31px Zapf-Humanist serif. Weights stay at 400 except an 18px/700 footer heading and a 14px/700 inline link. Letter-spacing is 0. Button padding is `13px 24px 12px` (1px more above the label than below). Primary button is graphite fill, cream label, 48px tall, square. Secondary is cream with a 1px ink border. — same source
- Aesop refuses, in the extractor’s words and counts: rounded commerce corners (0px everywhere except avatar dots at 50% and a 20px radius on video controls), drop shadows on product chrome, gradients, and saturated button color. Photography is amber glass, ceramic, stone, and partial hands, not face crops. This is an interpretation of the public site, updated 13 May 2026, not an Aesop brand book. Occurrence counts are the extractor’s. — same source
- GOAT, per ScreensDesign only: “minimalist black-and-white” with product photography in front. No hex, type, radius, or icon weight was stated. — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/goat-sneakers-apparel)
- Shop, per the 11 Dec 2024 changelog: merchant brand colors, imagery, and video enter the home feed. That is the opposite of a single-accent system: each card can bring its own color. No corner or type rules were published. — [Shopify Changelog](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/shop-app-has-a-new-merchant-focused-home-feed)
- Apple Store, Nov 2025: Liquid Glass on the bottom bar, icon, buttons, and menus. 9to5Mac notes the release notes for v6.6 say only “various improvements and performance enhancements,” and that some of the interface was not fully converted. This is a material system, explicitly out of scope as a layout direction. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/13/apple-store-app-updated-liquid-glass/)
- No opened source gave hex, type roles, corner radius, stroke, or icon weight for Zara, SNKRS, SSENSE, StockX, Depop, Mr Porter, Arc’teryx, or TOTEME. TOTEME’s vendor says “elegant fonts,” “generous white space,” and “full-bleed imagery” without naming a face or a color. — [Venn Apps](https://www.vennapps.com/case-studies/toteme)

### Inferences
- Aesop’s scarcity rule is the useful style lesson: one serif, one leather-colored hairline, no shadow. Warmth is assigned to the photograph. A second accent color would break the system the extraction describes.
- Shop’s 2024–2026 feed does the reverse on purpose: brand color is content, not chrome. A single accent would fight the feed.

### Gaps
- Aesop’s mobile navigation pattern (whether Skin / Hair / Body / Home / Read collapses) is not in the extraction. Cart, checkout, and account chrome were explicitly not extracted.
- Suisse Intl and Zapf-Humanist are licensed; the DESIGN.md’s substitutes (Söhne or Inter; Optima) are the writer’s, not Aesop’s.
- A live mobile screenshot of aesop.com was not opened separately from the DESIGN.md, which includes a full-page capture but describes the mobile rail in one line.

## Navigation, bottom action, safe areas, and density

### Takeaway
Where navigation is documented, it is a short fixed bottom bar or a thin top bar, and the dense or calm feeling comes from what the bar opens, not from extra chrome. Safe areas and touch-target specs are almost entirely missing. Aesop is the only measured control: a 48px square button and an 11px price.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Store: five bottom destinations — For You, Products, Go Further, Search, Bag — reported as the bar in India in Jan 2025 and as the Liquid Glass bar in Nov 2025. Jul 2024 is when Products replaced Shop and Go Further replaced Sessions. — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/prakharkhanna/2025/01/17/apple-strengthens-india-retail-presence-with-apple-store-app-launch/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/07/16/apple-store-app-overhaul/)
- Zara: MWM’s screenshot captions call the bottom bar minimal and “ever-present” on the campaign home and on collection screens. A Korean review quoted on that same page says the sold-out label is a heavy word centered on a black dim, and that a selected size filter is only a bold weight, so the selected state is hard to see. — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/zara/547951480)
- Aesop: top nav, 51px, cream, no border, wordmark left, links, search, bag, sign-in. Not a bottom tab bar in the extraction. Primary button height 48px. Section padding `40px 0 30px` is described as denser than a 96px marketing stack and aimed at scanning several product rails. Mobile rail is one product wide, which is calmer than the desktop 3-up but still a continuous index. — [Aesop DESIGN.md](https://www.shadcn.io/design/aesop)
- GOAT: no tab list in the opened teardown. Named surfaces are product (buy or offer), Wants, Owns, Inspiration, and a prominent Sell. Offer UI adds data (Top Offer, Last Sale) rather than more navigation. — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/goat-sneakers-apparel)
- SNKRS: the opened listing does not name tabs. It does say the shopper prepares a profile (name, size, address) and then meets a launch. Maps is an extra destination (partner shops, events, Nike stores), expanded in the 23 Oct 2026 notes. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nike.snkrs&hl=en_US)
- Shop, Feb 2026, as observed by Shopifreaks: a permanently visible back control on the home feed at the bottom left “that leads to nowhere,” and the writer could not find the search field. That is one reporter’s session, not a Shopify spec. — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/shopify-redesigns-the-shop-app-home-feed-for-a-video-centric-swipeable-shopping-experience/)
- StockX consumer density is the pair of actions plus market context, not a long spec sheet. Pro’s seller panel is explicitly data-dense (graph, spread, count of bids and asks at each price). Pro is not the consumer phone layout. — [Pro market data](https://stockx.com/news/how-to-view-market-data-on-stockx-pro/)
- Depop Outfits adds a scissors control on the right of a listing. The collage itself is a low-chrome canvas (background, place, template). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/depop-launches-a-fashion-collaging-tool-to-style-pinterest-worthy-outfits/)

### Inferences
- Calm versus rich is not a theme setting. Aesop is calm because type, rule, and one product per mobile row do all the work. StockX and GOAT are rich because the second price is the content. Zara’s campaign home can be visually rich while the bar stays minimal; reviewers still call the home busy.
- A bottom bar that never changes (Zara, Apple Store) is what keeps a campaign screen and a bag in the same app.

### Gaps
- No opened source specified safe-area padding, home-indicator clearance, or a sticky add-to-bag bar’s height for Zara, SSENSE, SNKRS, GOAT, StockX, Depop, Shop, Mr Porter, or TOTEME.
- SNKRS and SSENSE tab structures were not on the pages opened. Uptodown’s SNKRS tab names are unverified.

## How home, product, and a secondary screen stay one app

### Takeaway
The products that hold together reuse the same object (the listing, the look, the launch) in a different composition. They do not introduce a new visual language for bag, account, or story. Where a secondary screen was actually described, it is either a market action, a collage, a journal, a concierge, or a post-purchase tab.

### Cited Findings
- Aesop. Home: hero band plus category rails and a single serif section title. Product tile: image, then title, then an 11px price, square, hairline. The extraction does not document cart or account, and says those were not captured. The system implies they would use the same ink, paper, and square button. — [Aesop DESIGN.md](https://www.shadcn.io/design/aesop)
- StockX. Home is search into a product. The product page is the market (Offer or Buy Now). A secondary mode is not a story: it is another marketplace — Listings (condition and price vary) or, on US iOS, Live (real-time auction). The about page says some Live items start at $1. The screen layout of Live was not opened. Account and order status were not described. — [how to buy](https://stockx.com/help/articles/how-do-i-buy-on-stockx); [about buying](https://stockx.com/about/buying/)
- GOAT. Product page is photo plus buy or offer, and Sell is on that page. Wants and Owns are collection lists of the same products. Inspiration is editorial photography with the items shoppable from the photo. ScreensDesign says this is one black-and-white system. Bag/checkout is where the recording found friction (cart disabled when the price moves). — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/goat-sneakers-apparel)
- SNKRS. Home behavior, in Nike’s words, is stories, polls, maps, and launches. The “product page” is often an access mechanic (Pass, scratch, AR, geo) rather than a description page. Profile is the fixed size and address used at launch. A separate Nike shopping catalog is not what this listing describes. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nike.snkrs&hl=en_US)
- Depop. The shop remains listings. Outfits is a second composition of those listings (collage), entered from the listing, and still shoppable. TechCrunch explicitly compares it to Pinterest’s Shuffles. That is the risk of the secondary screen becoming another app; Depop’s mitigation, as stated, is that every piece in the collage is a Depop listing and sold-out pieces get alternatives. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/depop-launches-a-fashion-collaging-tool-to-style-pinterest-worthy-outfits/)
- Shop. Home is the merchant-branded or video swipe. Shopify’s changelog does not describe the product page or the bag. Shopifreaks’ critique is that the home no longer feels like a place you search from. — [Changelog](https://changelog.shopify.com/posts/shop-app-has-a-new-merchant-focused-home-feed); [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/shopify-redesigns-the-shop-app-home-feed-for-a-video-centric-swipeable-shopping-experience/)
- Zara. Home and collection screens are campaign imagery with the same bottom bar. MWM says a product page shows the garment and a price. Favorites, order tracking, returns, store finder, and an in-store QR (e-receipts, in-store returns, pickup) are listing features, not a second visual system. The bag’s layout was not described in the re-fetched MWM text. — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/zara/547951480); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.inditex.zara&hl=en_US)
- SSENSE. Home modules (browsed brands, recently viewed, wishlist, category spotlights) and the product page (full look, similar styles, and on the live site a “Styled with” group) use the same catalog object. Order history and wishlist are named in the Play text as the account-side screens. Their visual layout was not opened. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ssense.android.prod&hl=en_US)
- Mr Porter. Shop and The Journal are both in the app; the Journal is “style news to go” that can be shopped. Wish list is shared. No account-screen description was opened. — [app page](https://www.mrporter.com/en-mx/content/app/)
- Apple Store. For You (saved items, recent activity, offers), Products (hardware and compatible accessories), Go Further (setup, tips, Today at Apple), Bag. The three content tabs are different jobs, same bar. Product-detail layout versus bag layout was not described. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/apple-store/id375380948/)
- TOTEME. Vendor says the shopping UI matches the website, Shop The Look is its own page and the highest-converting screen in the app, and ℅ TOTEME is a services page (booking, guides, concierge) rather than a points wallet. Bag layout not described. — [Venn Apps](https://www.vennapps.com/case-studies/toteme)

### Inferences
- A secondary screen stays in-app when it is another arrangement of the same SKU (styled-with, shop the look, collage, journal) or another verb on the same SKU (offer, sell, reserve). It drifts when it becomes a freeform art tool (Depop’s color canvas) or a video feed with no search (Shop, as one observer saw it).
- Apple’s Go Further tab is the cleanest example of a non-shopping screen that still belongs: it is about products you already bought, on the same bar.

### Gaps
- Bag and account screens were not opened for any app except by feature name. Visual continuity into checkout is unverified everywhere except GOAT’s noted cart exception and Aesop’s undescribed cart.
- SSENSE “full-look” and “70 percent of the page is the image” must not be merged. The 70 percent figure comes from a Fibre2Fashion item whose body matches the original app launch (about 40,000 items and 500 brands) while current listings say 60,000-plus items and 700 brands. It was not used as a 2024–2026 measurement. — search extract of [Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/news/textiles-technology-news/ssense-unveils-mobile-app-for-hyper-personalised-shopping-252870-newsdetails.htm); current scope in [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=ssense.android.prod&hl=en_US)

## What are the risks: readability, type size, clutter, trend decay, contrast, touch, motion?

### Takeaway
The documented failures are gesture conflicts, type and contrast that hide the garment or the selected control, a market price that disables the familiar cart, and a video home that ages like other shopping-only video feeds. Tiny measured type shows up only at Aesop, where prices are specified at 11px.

### Cited Findings
- Aesop: price and meta labels are specified at 11px with line-height 1.2. Body is 14px. The 48px button is a normal touch height; the 11px price is the readability risk, especially on a 1-up mobile rail where the price is the only commerce text under the photo. Caption color is not separately specified beyond quiet gray `#999999` for disabled and placeholder text. — [Aesop DESIGN.md](https://www.shadcn.io/design/aesop)
- Zara gesture and clutter: a Play review dated 18 Jul 2026 in the search extract says photos advance by swiping down, the swipe is sensitive enough to go sideways instead, and the home is “far too busy.” Another review in that extract says a horizontal swipe leaves the product for the next one, and the writer got stuck between two product pages. A Korean review on MWM says the sold-out stamp covers the garment, and the selected size filter lacks contrast (bold weight only). These are user reports, not Inditex documentation. The 2018 review that already asked for full-bleed photos and complained about vertical swapping is historical context: the gesture risk is old. — search extract of [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.inditex.zara&hl=en_US); [MWM review](https://mwm.ai/apps/zara/547951480)
- Zara performance, same MWM review summary: slow product images, frequent updates, and a filter selected-state that is hard to see. Not a layout invention, but it is how a campaign-heavy home fails. — [MWM](https://mwm.ai/apps/zara/547951480)
- GOAT: ScreensDesign says Add to Cart can be disabled because the price moves quickly, pushing the shopper into Checkout. Two prices plus a disabled cart is a comprehension risk, not a visual-trend risk. The circular offer-duration control is a small, unusual touch target; its pixel size was not given. — [ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/goat-sneakers-apparel)
- StockX: Buy Now and Make Offer (older help still says Bid) must stay distinct. Regional all-in pricing means a buyer’s lowest Ask can differ from a sale printed in history, which StockX explains as duties in the Ask. A chart of “last sale” next to “your price” can look inconsistent even when the market is working. That is a labeling risk on any market layout. — [Make Offer vs Buy Now](https://stockx.com/help/articles/What-is-the-difference-between-Make-Offer-and-Buy-Now-when-purchasing); [why a sale can exceed an Ask](https://stockx.com/help/articles/why-did-an-item-sell-for-higher-than-my-asking-price)
- SNKRS: Play reviews on the page opened (one dated March 2025 in the earlier search extract) say drop notifications and the purchase path fail under load. Nike’s own listing makes the buy path conditional on scratch, AR, or location. That is a discovery risk: the product can be hidden by the launch mechanic. Icon personalization in Oct 2026 does not fix that. No motion-spec was published. — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.nike.snkrs&hl=en_US)
- Shop trend decay: Shopifreaks (9 Feb 2026) compares the swipe video home to Amazon Inspire, which it says launched in Sep 2022 and later shut down, and reports that the videos actually seen were product clips or a cropped cover behind a carousel, plus a back button that does nothing and a missing search. The Inspire shutdown and the “meh” judgment are that publication’s, not Shopify’s. The structural risk it names is real if search is gone: a feed with no other job than shopping. — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/shopify-redesigns-the-shop-app-home-feed-for-a-video-centric-swipeable-shopping-experience/)
- Depop: Outfits invites color backgrounds and free placement. TechCrunch frames collages as a Gen Z trend shared with Pinterest Shuffles (2022, brought into the main Pinterest app in 2023). A colorful canvas next to a square shop grid can date faster than the grid, and can make the garment secondary to the board. Sold-out substitution is Depop’s stated guardrail. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/24/depop-launches-a-fashion-collaging-tool-to-style-pinterest-worthy-outfits/)
- Apple Liquid Glass (Nov 2025) is the trend-decay case inside this set: a system-wide material applied to an existing tab bar. It does not document a contrast or touch failure, and it should not be copied as the layout idea. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/13/apple-store-app-updated-liquid-glass/)
- SSENSE business context, not a UI measurement: an October 2025 nss magazine piece treats SSENSE’s restructuring as a 2024–2025 luxury e-commerce failure and quotes a criticism that mixing luxury with streetwear plus constant discounts overloaded the assortment. That is merchandising clutter, not a measured type or contrast failure. — [nss magazine, 3 Oct 2025](https://www.nssmag.com/en/fashion/42836/ssense-collapse-luxury-ecommerce-crisis-fashion-retail-2025)

### Inferences
- The durable risks are structural: a gesture that changes product when the thumb meant to change photo (Zara reviews), a second price that removes the cart (GOAT), an 11px price (Aesop spec), and a home that is only a video swipe (Shop, as observed). Decorative trends (glass, collage color, custom icons) are easier to date and were not the layout.
- A market page needs both prices labeled in the same units, or it looks broken. StockX’s own help exists because last-sale and lowest-ask are easy to misread.

### Gaps
- No contrast ratios, no WCAG notes, and no motion durations were published by Zara, Nike, SSENSE, StockX, GOAT, Depop, Mr Porter, Apple, or TOTEME. Aesop’s motion note is limited: 200ms micro, 250ms small, 500ms medium, and a cubic-bezier, from the OpenDesign extract of the same site, which is a second unofficial spec and was not fully re-fetched. — [OpenDesign Aesop](https://opendesign.cc/en/sites/aesop)
- Touch-target failures are inferred only where a review or teardown describes a miss (Zara’s sideways swipe, GOAT’s disabled cart). Minimum 44px targets were not measured.
- Reformation, Farfetch, Glossier, COS, and Arc’teryx have no risk notes because no distinctive current layout was verified to assess.
