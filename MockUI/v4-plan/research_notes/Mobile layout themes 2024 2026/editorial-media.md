# Editorial, media, and culture mobile layout themes, 2024–2026-10-31

Scope: structural arrangement (how content is stacked, how the eye moves, how home / detail / secondary screens differ). Not skins. Cutoff for “current” is 2026-10-31. Claims without a source I opened are in Gaps. Spec redesigns on Dribbble, Behance, and student portfolios are not treated as the live product.

Products checked: The New York Times app, Apple News (app chrome and Apple News Format articles), MUBI, Letterboxd, Substack, The Atlantic, Monocle (the magazine), Criterion Channel, Readwise Reader, Matter, Apple Music, Spotify mobile Now Playing.

## Which live products use a layout idea beyond a restyled card stack, and what is the primary record?

### Takeaway

Six live products have a documented structural move in 2024–2026: The New York Times (a sideways ribbon of different homes over a fixed mode bar), Apple News Format (a column canvas that publishers can break for full-bleed and overlap — this is the article, not the Today chrome), Letterboxd Android (a bottom bar whose center slot is the log action, with reviews pulled off the film screen), Substack (a subscribed queue sharing Home with a social feed, plus a classic-versus-dense inbox), Spotify Now Playing (queue and then lyrics pinned under the artwork), and Readwise Reader (a library that can shed its action bar when the document is a book). The Atlantic’s 27 October 2026 app update is the clearest screenshot-backed case: one masthead system, then a screen that is allowed to become a photo essay, an author column, a color-block games shelf, or an audio stage. MUBI, Monocle magazine, Criterion’s app chrome, Matter, and Apple Music’s in-app layout were not verified to that standard.

### Inferences

- The repeatable 2024–2026 move in this set is not a new card. It is a fixed frame (mode bar, masthead, or reading column) with permission for one region to change job: NYT’s ribbon, Atlantic’s first screen, Spotify’s stage under the art, Reader’s decision to delete the bottom bar.
- Apple News the product and Apple News Format the article system should not be collapsed. The Format docs are the only Apple-authored layout grammar found. The Today screen’s 2025–2026 changes are navigation density, reported by trade press.
- Letterboxd’s verified 2025 change is navigation and information architecture on the film screen, not a new poster system. Poster grids are the expected Letterboxd picture, but this pass did not open a screenshot that shows them.

## What is the one structural move, what stays fixed, and what may change?

### Takeaway

Each verified product keeps one spine and lets a single zone change. The spine is the bottom mode bar (Times, Atlantic, Letterboxd, Spotify, Reader), the article column (Apple News Format, Reader’s reading view), or the masthead-plus-tab pair (Atlantic). The zone that changes is the sideways “which home” (Times), the hero treatment (Atlantic), the stage under the artwork (Spotify), or whether chrome is allowed on the page at all (Reader long-form, News Format full-bleed).

### Cited Findings

- **Times — sideways homes, fixed modes.** Fixed: you still open on Today; bottom modes are how you read, hear, play, or personalize (Home, Listen, Play, You in Nieman and WAN-IFRA; Fast Company says that bar barely moved). Changes: the top ribbon is a row of alternate front pages, journalism one direction and other products the other. Lifestyle is allowed a more photographic treatment than Today. Sub-brand sections are samples that hand off to other apps, so those screens are not allowed to become the full Cooking or Athletic product. — [NYT Co.](https://www.nytco.com/press/our-new-times-app/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91199198/the-new-york-times-app-redesign); [Nieman](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/10/the-new-york-times-redesigns-its-app-to-highlight-a-universe-beyond-just-news/); [WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/read-play-swipe-the-strategy-behind-the-new-york-times-app-revamp/)
- **Apple News Format — column grid, with licensed breaks.** Fixed: components align to the same column count, gutter, and margin, and that grid scales by growing margin rather than adding columns. Allowed to change: a component may ignore viewport padding (full-bleed) or the document margin; a header may be layered and then covered by the body (parallax overlap); a caption may sit in the side margin; a pull quote or photo may be inset with text wrap. Role of the piece (title, body, caption, quote, image) stays a named component. — [Planning](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/planning-the-layout-for-your-article); [Positioning](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/positioning-the-content-in-your-article); [Tutorials](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/apple-news-format-tutorials)
- **Letterboxd Android — the log action is the center of the bar.** Fixed after August 2025: bottom bar, center slot is log/review, Profile holds settings, film facts stay on the film screen. Allowed to move: reviews, which leave the film screen for one gray tile; cast, which becomes a vertical list instead of whatever horizontal treatment preceded it. — [Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/crew/story/huge-update-to-our-android-app-is-out-now/)
- **Substack — one Home, two reading densities.** Fixed: Home is both “your subscriptions’ posts” and a notes feed; Read/Latest is the chronological queue; archive is a left swipe, not a new visual system. Allowed to change: classic inbox shows preview images; dense inbox drops them and shrinks title and subtitle. Profile is a menu, not a third feed design. — [Substack Help](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/19291693034004-Getting-started-on-the-Substack-app)
- **Spotify — the Now Playing stage is artwork, then words, then queue.** Fixed: album art or Canvas remains the top of Now Playing; the three-dot menu still dismisses lyric previews; Queue remains the bottom control of that view (May 2025) even after lyrics move up under the art (February 2026). Allowed to change: whether words are under the art, and whether the queue reveals recommended tracks after yours. Library stays a bottom destination, not a second now-playing. — [Spotify, May 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-05-07/experience-a-new-dimension-of-music-discovery-with-more-controls-and-enhanced-tools/); [Spotify, February 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-02-04/lyric-translations-offline-previews/)
- **Readwise Reader — the library keeps tools; the book is allowed to lose them.** Fixed: bottom navigation includes Views; documents are a list with status dots and a progress line; reading progress never moves backward. Allowed to change, by document type: long-form hides the bottom action bar; paged scroll replaces continuous scroll; justification is optional; Home can be skipped so the app opens in the current document. — [FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/); [Long-form guide](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/guides/workflows/longform-reading)
- **Atlantic, from the four opened frames (27 October 2026).** Fixed across Home, Author, and Games: a white ground, a centered high-contrast serif for the screen title (wordmark, AUTHOR, GAMES), and a bottom mode bar labeled Home, Latest, Audio, Games. Home’s active state is a red “A” and a red Home label inside a lighter pill; Games uses a red glyph when selected. Allowed to change completely: Home is a full-bleed photograph under the masthead, then one headline block, then the top of a second story. Author is a centered portrait column, then a rule and the word LATEST, then a full-width photo, with a mini player over the bottom. Games abandons the quiet page for three stacked, saturated, rounded panels. The audio sheet abandons the tab bar for a 2×2 color stage and a white control column. — [home shot](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrEfMhbIAACBUk.jpg); [author shot](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrEjgLakAAIx-e.jpg); [games shot](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrEpspbcAAUeIF.jpg); [audio shot](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrFERAasAAGRdO.jpg)

### Inferences

- “More than a card stack” here means the first screen is a sequence of roles (masthead, picture, headline, byline, next story) or a sequence of modes, not a repeated container. Atlantic Home is the cleanest example because the lead story has no visible card edge; the photograph is the screen width.
- The Times ribbon and the Atlantic bottom bar solve the same problem (news plus games plus audio) with opposite axes. Times moves between journalism and products sideways and keeps consumption modes at the bottom. Atlantic puts consumption modes at the bottom (Home, Latest, Audio, Games) and uses story size, not a second axis, to vary Home. Both refuse to become one undifferentiated feed.
- Reader’s long-form mode is the strict version of the same idea: when the task is reading, the tools that define the library are not allowed to stay.

### Gaps

- Times article template (how a story page differs from Today) was not opened. The press material is about the front, not the story.
- Spotify home shelves versus Now Playing were not opened; only the Now Playing and bottom-bar sentences in the two newsroom posts.
- Whether Atlantic Home continues as a single column of alternating sizes, or also uses the carousels Thompson mentions, cannot be told from one frame. He says carousels and section headers exist; the opened Home frame does not show a carousel.

## Palette, type, corners, strokes, imagery, icons — only what was opened — and what the system refuses

### Takeaway

The only screen-level visual system verified by opened images is The Atlantic’s October 2026 app: white paper, one serif for titles, red as the only functional color on reading screens, full-bleed photography with a small credit, and a deliberate break into flat color on Games and on the audio stage. Everywhere else, color and type are either a maker’s words (Spotify Mix, News Format legibility, Reader’s blue-to-purple progress) or not safe to specify.

Below, on white: serif headline (truncated in the frame), byline in small caps or tracked small type, a red scrubber, black transport icons (1.0×, 15-second skips, pause, skip). “Playing Next” is a plain list: small thumbnail, title, “58m • DAVID BROOKS” style meta, a grip. Games is the exception that proves the rule: color is reserved for a mode that is not reading.
- **Times, words only:** Lifestyle is “visual” because the photography earns it; Today stays text-heavy. ### Inferences

- Atlantic’s refusal is “color is a section, not a decoration.” Reading screens stay paper-and-ink. Games and the audio stage may be posters. A designer copying this should not add accent color to the article column.
- News Format’s refusal is the reverse of a free collage: you may overlap and bleed only as a component on the grid. The system refuses an unaligned composition and, on very wide screens, refuses letting images grow without limit.
- Do not import the third-party MUBI tokens (zero radius, cobalt, Riforma) into a mobile recommendation. They were not verified on the app, and the page that lists them is not MUBI’s.

- No opened screenshot for Times, News app chrome, MUBI, Letterboxd, Substack, Reader, Criterion, or Spotify, so corners, strokes, and icon weight for those products are unknown.
- Atlantic type is “a serif” in the frames. The family name was not identified. Dark Mode is documented in the help center and was not in the screenshots, which are all light.
- Apple Music’s artwork-tinted track list and any black-and-white 2026 rebrand were not seen.

## Navigation and density: bottom zone, safe areas, calm versus rich

### Takeaway

Calm and rich are assigned to different screens, not mixed in one chrome. Reading stays sparse (Atlantic Home and Author, Reader long-form, Times Today as the text-heavy center). Richness is a named destination (Atlantic Games, Times Lifestyle and Play, Spotify’s lyric line and queue). The bottom of the phone is a mode switch or a player, and several of these products let that zone cover content.

### Cited Findings

- **Times.** Bottom bar is the mode switch: Home discovers journalism, Listen is audio, Play is games, You is personalization that has to explain itself (the old For You did not). The ribbon is a second, horizontal navigation that can put the most popular sections at the far ends so people travel. Dudish: the news app should be a launchpad, not a super app. Withrow: games and news send people to each other. — [WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/read-play-swipe-the-strategy-behind-the-new-york-times-app-revamp/); [Pugpig](https://www.pugpig.com/2025/05/16/how-the-new-york-times-updated-app-supports-its-subscription-bundle-business/); [Nieman](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/10/the-new-york-times-redesigns-its-app-to-highlight-a-universe-beyond-just-news/)
- **Atlantic, opened frames.** Primary tabs are Home, Latest, Audio, Games, in a rounded bar over the content. Home is calm: one photograph and one story, with only the top of the next story visible, so density is low on purpose. Games is rich: three large colored panels and a row of dates, with a further title already under the bar. Author keeps the calm column but adds a persistent mini player (thumbnail, title, “Tom Bartlett • 7:03,” pause, a circular progress mark) over the photograph. The full audio sheet replaces the tab bar with transport, a queue, and two black buttons. — [four screenshots in the thread](https://x.com/nxthompson/status/2115220789977067642)
- **Letterboxd.** Density on the film screen went down: reviews are no longer in the main column; cast gets vertical room “easier on your thumbs.” The bottom bar absorbs the old floating log button, so the thumb’s primary action is centered rather than floating over the poster. Settings are one level down, on Profile, not in the bar. — [Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/crew/story/huge-update-to-our-android-app-is-out-now/)
- **Substack.** Home is the dense surface (queue plus notes, likes, comments, recommendations). Read/Latest is the calmer chronological list, and the dense inbox setting removes images. Archive is a swipe, so the row itself stays a single action target. Chat is off the reading path. — [Substack Help](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/19291693034004-Getting-started-on-the-Substack-app)
- **Spotify.** Now Playing grows a lyric line under the art and keeps Queue at the bottom of that view, so the player is a tall stack, not a bar only. Create is a bottom-right “+,” separate from the bottom destinations; Library is the third bottom item. Lyric previews can be turned off, which is an admission that the richer player is optional. — [May 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-05-07/experience-a-new-dimension-of-music-discovery-with-more-controls-and-enhanced-tools/); [February 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-02-04/lyric-translations-offline-previews/)
- **Reader.** Standard mobile reading keeps a bottom toolbar and a thin progress line just above it. Long-form hides that action bar. The docs show the cost: the listen button is no longer where it was, and the reader must open “…”. A Return control can also sit near the bottom and can be hidden without stopping tracking. Home can be avoided entirely. — [FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/); [Long-form guide](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/guides/workflows/longform-reading)
- **Apple News chrome, partial.** Current Apple Support documents Following as a place to edit favorites, block, and notifications, which is a management screen rather than another magazine. 9to5Mac’s iOS 26.2 account adds four section links above Today and frees the bottom bar for Following. Stuff’s June 2026 list (Today, News+, Audio, Following, Search) is five bottom items. Safe-area measurements were not published. — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/iphone/iph5b557ed3d/ios); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/04/ios-26-2s-apple-news-app-has-a-new-and-improved-design/); [Stuff](https://www.stuff.tv/features/apple-news-how-use-it/)
- **Criterion, partial.** Settings live at the lower right, not in a labeled mode bar that the help page names. Explore is the tab that can show or hide Resume. Light and dark are a user switch. — [Criterion Android help](https://www.criterionchannel.com/help/using-the-android-app/how-do-i-change-the-android-app-settings)
- **Apple Music, trade press only.** AppleInsider says the iOS 26 bar collapses while scrolling so the now-playing capsule and one icon share a shorter bottom zone. That is a density behavior, not confirmed on an Apple page. — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/inside/apple-music/vs/apple-music-ios-26-vs-ios-18-automix-liquid-glass-and-lyrics-in-your-language)

### Inferences

- A calm editorial home in this set is one dominant story plus a visible hint of the next, not a long stack of equal cards. Atlantic Home and Times Today (as described) both do this. Richness is opt-in by section or by a preview line (lyrics, games panels).
- The bottom zone is doing three incompatible jobs across the set, and the better examples pick one: mode switch (Times, Atlantic, Letterboxd), player (Spotify, Atlantic mini player), or actions on the document (Reader). Atlantic shows the collision: the mini player and the mode bar both want the bottom, and the screenshots show them covering type and pictures.
- “Safe area” is not specified by any maker page opened. The practical evidence is overlap: Atlantic’s bar and mini player sit on top of content; Letterboxd moved a floating button into the bar so it would stop covering the film.

### Gaps

- No measured margins, tab heights, or hit targets for any product.
- Times Listen versus the ribbon’s audio entry is described as two doors to similar content. How those two screens differ visually was not opened.
- Substack bottom-tab labels are not fully listed in the help article (Home and Read are; Chat is a section; the bar’s other icons are not named in the text fetched).

## How home, detail, and a secondary screen differ

### Takeaway

Where more than one screen was documented, home is the curated or personal front, detail drops chrome or changes axis, and the secondary screen is either a tool (games, queue, profile, following) or a list (latest, reviews, cast). The Atlantic set is the only one where all three were seen. The others are known from maker text for two of the three.

### Cited Findings

- **Atlantic, seen.** Home: masthead, one full-bleed photo, one complete story block, the start of a second story, mode bar. Detail-equivalent author screen: no masthead, a back chevron, a centered identity column, then Latest as a new stack of full-width stories, plus the mini player. Secondary Games: same title position as Author, but the body is colored posters and date chips instead of prose. Secondary audio: not a tab at all; it is a sheet with art stage, transport, and queue, and it offers “Read Story” back into the article. Latest is a tab in the bar and was not opened, so its layout is unknown. Thompson says Home also has carousels and more story sizes than the one frame shows. — [thread and images](https://x.com/nxthompson/status/2115220789977067642)
- **Times, described.** Home is Today, text-led, urgent. The next swipe (Lifestyle) is the visual home. Great Reads and Opinion are further homes, not filters of Today. Detail (the article) is not described in the launch posts. Secondary: Listen, Play, and You are different jobs. You is the one that learns (a column read repeatedly is added) and is explicitly not a mystery algorithmic mix. Games inside the news app are a preview, not the full Games app. — [NYT Co.](https://www.nytco.com/press/our-new-times-app/); [WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/read-play-swipe-the-strategy-behind-the-new-york-times-app-revamp/); [Nieman](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/10/the-new-york-times-redesigns-its-app-to-highlight-a-universe-beyond-just-news/)
- **Letterboxd, described.** The film screen is the detail: ratings chart and watch rows stay; reviews do not, they are one tile that leads to a Reviews section; cast is its own vertical scroll. Profile is the secondary screen and now owns Settings. Search is a tab that focuses on a second tap. Home / diary layout is not in the post. — [Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/crew/story/huge-update-to-our-android-app-is-out-now/)
- **Substack, described.** Home mixes the subscription queue with notes. Read is the secondary list: chronological, saved, paid, swipe to archive, classic or dense. Opening a post is implied (the queue exists to be read) but the article layout is not specified. After a post, a November 2025 third-party newsletter says Notes appear under the finished post; that is not in the help article opened here. — [Substack Help](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/19291693034004-Getting-started-on-the-Substack-app); [Andi Bitay, 29 November 2025](https://ditchthetemplates.substack.com/p/substack-just-became-more-like-instagram)
- **Reader, described.** Home is a configurable list (pinned views such as Books). Detail is the document: standard toolbar, or long-form / paged / justified. A secondary list is any View, including Trash at the bottom of Search or the View tab. The structural jump is list-with-dots to a page that may have no bottom bar. — [FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/); [Long-form guide](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/guides/workflows/longform-reading)
- **Spotify, described.** Now Playing is the detail: art or Canvas, lyric line directly under it, queue at the bottom of the view. The rest of the app is not specified except that playlists gained Add / Sort / Edit at the top (May 2025) and Library is a bottom tab. Home shelves were not described in the posts opened. — [May 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-05-07/experience-a-new-dimension-of-music-discovery-with-more-controls-and-enhanced-tools/); [February 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-02-04/lyric-translations-offline-previews/)
- **Apple News Format** is only the detail. The tutorials’ “home” does not exist; the article opens on a header that can be image-led, then a column of body, with optional side caption and inset quote. The News app’s Today versus Following split is feed versus management, from Apple Support, not a visual comparison. — [Tutorials](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/apple-news-format-tutorials); [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/iphone/iph5b557ed3d/ios)
- **Criterion.** Explore is the named home-like tab (Resume can sit at its top). Title and player screens were not described. Miller’s square asset is what mobile “uses,” which implies the phone hero is square while TV is wide, but the surrounding UI was not shown. — [Android help](https://www.criterionchannel.com/help/using-the-android-app/how-do-i-change-the-android-app-settings); [Miller](https://www.fronmiller.com/fron-miller/2025/8/21/the-criterion-channel-graphics)

### Inferences

- A workable pattern for an editorial app that also has audio and games: keep the article and the author page on paper, and let only the named secondary (games, player) change materials. Atlantic does this. The Times does the editorial version with a swipe instead of a material change: Today stays the serious front, Lifestyle is allowed to be the picture front, Play is a different mode rather than a block inside Today.
- Detail should not repeat home’s navigation chrome if the job is reading or logging. Reader hides the bar. Letterboxd keeps the bar (the log action still matters on a film) but removes the social reviews from the first screen.

### Gaps

- No opened article page for Times, Atlantic (the audio sheet has “Read Story” but the story itself was not shown), Substack, or News.
- Letterboxd home versus film versus profile was not seen, only described in part.
- Apple Music album screen versus Now Playing versus Library was not seen. 9to5Mac’s “fullscreen album” note is not a screen opened here.

## Risks: readability, small type, clutter, trend decay, contrast, touch size, motion

### Takeaway

The documented risks are overlap (chrome covering type), a second navigation that hides sections, a richer player that has to be switched off, and a reading mode that hides the listen button. Trend-shaped risks that are visible in the Atlantic frames are clipped controls, a hero so tall that the next story is only a headline, and saturated panels next to a quiet magazine. No source gave contrast ratios or minimum hit sizes.

### Cited Findings

- **Times — finding things versus a second map.** Withrow’s stated problem is abundance: important stories leave the front because something else replaces them. The remedy is more fronts, which Pugpig notes are arranged so puzzles and opinion sit at the far ends. That only works if people learn the ribbon. Fast Company says the redesign was visually familiar on purpose, which lowers shock and also means the new structure can be missed. Dudish and Nieman both stress it must not become a super app or an unexplained algorithmic For You; the You tab exists because the old personal front failed clarity. Click-counting was rejected because it “can lead to clickbait-type behaviour”; they count 30-second engaged clicks instead. Looping video in Lifestyle was a plan in June 2025, not a shown result; if it shipped later, this note did not verify it. — [WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/read-play-swipe-the-strategy-behind-the-new-york-times-app-revamp/); [Pugpig](https://www.pugpig.com/2025/05/16/how-the-new-york-times-updated-app-supports-its-subscription-bundle-business/); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91199198/the-new-york-times-app-redesign); [Nieman](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/10/the-new-york-times-redesigns-its-app-to-highlight-a-universe-beyond-just-news/)
- **News Format — scale and type on images.** Apple warns that fewer than five columns may not survive scaling to a phone. Full-bleed and layered headers are taught together with legibility color for type on photography. Parallax is defined as the body overlapping the header, which is a collision risk if the header still contains type. Side captions use margin that disappears or tightens on a phone; the docs say the grid’s job is to reflow that, not that every side caption remains side-by-side on iPhone. — [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenewsformat/layout); [Planning](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/planning-the-layout-for-your-article); [Tutorials](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/apple-news-format-tutorials)
- **Atlantic frames — what the pictures already show going wrong.** The lead photograph occupies most of the first screen; the second story is only a headline and a thumbnail above the bar, so a missed headline is easy. The Games date chips run off the panel, so the last day is not fully tappable in the frame. “Suspicious Incident” is under the tab bar. The audio title is cut (“Can’t Cu”). The author photo is partly behind the mini player. Red on white for Follow and the active tab is the contrast bet; the red scrubber on white is thin. Games’ pink, orange, and yellow are flat and high-chroma next to black type, which is readable in the frame but is a different standard from the article pages. Motion is not visible in the stills. Thompson says the app is “much faster,” which is performance, not animation. — [opened screenshots](https://x.com/nxthompson/status/2115220789977067642)
- **Help-center drift at The Atlantic.** The public iOS help still sends readers to a Search tab for sections and Magazine, while the 27 October 2026 screenshots show Home / Latest / Audio / Games and no Magazine control. A designer using the help page would document the wrong map. — [Atlantic Help](https://support.theatlantic.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038751393-The-Atlantic-s-iOS-App); [Play listing, 23 October 2026](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.theatlantic.newsstand&hl=en_US)
- **Letterboxd — thumb reach versus an extra tap.** Vertical cast is justified as thumb comfort and room for names. Pulling reviews off the film screen reduces clutter and adds a step (the gray tile). Putting log/review in the center of the bar removes a floating control that covered content, and concentrates the main action where a thumb already rests. Tablet users are explicitly left out of this Android update. Almost every screen changed; the post warns of bugs. — [Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/crew/story/huge-update-to-our-android-app-is-out-now/)
- **Substack — two densities, and a feed that is not the inbox.** The dense inbox exists because preview images make the list tall. Home is allowed to be a social feed (notes, likes, comments, recommendations), which the help text itself says can include people you do not subscribe to. That is a clutter risk next to a reading queue; mute and block are the documented escapes. The classic/dense switch is the maker’s own answer to visual noise. A vertical video tab, if the Engadget report is still accurate in late 2026, would be a third density the help article does not prepare anyone for. — [Substack Help](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/19291693034004-Getting-started-on-the-Substack-app); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/substack-updated-its-app-to-make-it-even-more-like-tiktok-193415204.html)
- **Spotify — type on a moving stage.** Lyric previews sit under art or a short Canvas clip. Canvas is motion. Spotify says tests improved lyric engagement and return plays, and also ships an off switch in the overflow menu, so the line is not considered mandatory. Translations add a second line under the first on the lyrics card, which lengthens an already stacked player (art, words, controls, queue). Queue was also redesigned in 2025 to add Shuffle, Smart Shuffle, Repeat, Sleep Timer, and recommended follow-on tracks. The risk is a player that becomes a dashboard. Spotify’s mitigation is the off switch, not a simpler default. The Verge’s 2024 Mix story is about identity, not readability; no size or contrast figure was given. — [February 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-02-04/lyric-translations-offline-previews/); [May 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-05-07/experience-a-new-dimension-of-music-discovery-with-more-controls-and-enhanced-tools/); [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2024/5/22/24162464/spotify-mix-typeface-font-announcement-design-update)
- **Reader — calm that hides the tool.** Long-form removes the bottom bar and, with it, the obvious listen button. The documentation’s own FAQ is “the play button is missing.” Paged scroll and justification chase a book and can make a ragged article look like one. The progress line is thin by design (“a thin line above the bottom toolbar”); no contrast value is given. Shake-to-undo on iOS is easy to miss. — [Long-form guide](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/guides/workflows/longform-reading); [FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/)
- **Apple Music, unverified in primary sources, flagged only as a risk if the trade press is right.** A track list whose ground is sampled from artwork (9to5Mac, March 2026) will sometimes fail contrast; the article already says the color is not always a direct sample. A tab bar that collapses on scroll (AppleInsider) hides destinations until you tap again. Both are secondary. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/03/25/apple-music-in-ios-26-4-has-new-design-for-albums-playlists-and-more/); [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/inside/apple-music/vs/apple-music-ios-26-vs-ios-18-automix-liquid-glass-and-lyrics-in-your-language)
- **Trend decay, stated as a boundary not a finding.** This pass did not find maker copy pushing glassmorphism, purple-blue gradients, or neon as the editorial idea. AppleInsider uses “Liquid Glass” for the iOS 26 Music chrome; that is platform chrome, and it was not confirmed on an Apple editorial-app page. Atlantic’s opened bar is rounded and translucent in the frames; calling it Liquid Glass would be an inference, not a label in Thompson’s posts.

### Inferences

- The readability failure mode in this set is not tiny body text. It is a tall image or a floating bar leaving too little of the next thing on screen, and a second line of type (deck, lyric translation, date chip) that gets clipped.
- Personalization is treated as a trust problem at the Times: if the reader cannot tell why an item is there, the layout has failed even if the card is handsome. That is more useful than a visual trend warning.
- A culture app that adds games or video should put them on their own screen or their own swipe, not in the reading column. The Times, Atlantic, and (if accurate) Substack all did a version of this. Mixing them is the clutter path their own IA is trying to avoid.

### Gaps

- No contrast ratios, type sizes in points, or hit-target measurements for any product.
- No opened motion study. Spotify Canvas-under-lyrics and the Times’ planned Lifestyle video are the only motion risks named, and the Times video was not confirmed as shipped.
- MUBI, Monocle, Criterion player, Matter, and Apple Music cannot be scored for readability from sources opened here.
- Whether the Atlantic Games chip clip and the truncated audio title are the real layout or an artifact of how the screenshot was framed cannot be proven. They are in the images the CEO posted, at a 9:41 status bar, so they are at least the marketing frames of the redesign.
