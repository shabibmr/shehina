# Platform and systems: layout change versus decoration, 2024–2026

Scope is Apple HIG (iOS 18 context, iOS 26 Liquid Glass, HIG pages current through 2026-10-31), Material 3 Expressive, one OEM only where a primary source shows a layout idea, and premium utility apps. Liquid Glass is recorded as a platform material, not as a theme the product should depend on. Pre-2023 behavior is labeled historical only when a current page’s changelog shows the behavior was not introduced in 2024–2026. No quotes were invented. Screenshot descriptions are Apple’s or Google’s page art, not captures made for this note.

## What actually changed in Apple’s HIG for tab bars, toolbars, sheets, large titles, safe areas, and materials — and which layout rules stayed mandatory?

### Takeaway
iOS 26 (announced 9 June 2025) changed the **container** for controls: tab bars and toolbars float on a separate Liquid Glass layer, can shrink on scroll, and group actions into glass clusters. The screen underneath is still a content column with a title, a navigation/action bar, and (on iPhone) a bottom tab bar. iPhone Duo (HIG page new 9 September 2026) is the real 2026 **layout** change: those same bars move to the side. Large titles, sheet detents, safe areas, 44 pt targets, contrast, and Dynamic Type were not replaced.

### Cited Findings
- Apple’s 9 June 2025 Newsroom post introduces Liquid Glass as a translucent material for controls, navigation, icons, and widgets. It states that in iOS 26 tab bars shrink while scrolling to focus content and expand again when the user scrolls back up, and that iPadOS and macOS sidebars were updated the same way. — [Apple Newsroom, 9 June 2025](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/)
- Current HIG Materials (page last-modified in the fetch as Thu, 29 Oct 2026): Apple platforms have two materials. Liquid Glass is “a distinct functional layer for controls and navigation elements — like tab bars and sidebars — that floats above the content layer.” Content may scroll and peek through. Standard materials (blur, vibrancy, blend) structure the content underneath. — [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)
- Same page, explicit refusal: “Don’t use Liquid Glass in the content layer.” Use standard materials for content, including app backgrounds. Exception: a transient control in the content layer (sliders, toggles) may take on Liquid Glass only while the person is activating it. Custom Liquid Glass should be rare; overuse “distracts from that content.” — [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)
- Two glass variants. **Regular** (default for most system components, including text-heavy alerts, sidebars, popovers) blurs and adjusts luminosity; scroll-edge effects further blur and fade background content. **Clear** is highly translucent and is only for components over visually rich media. If the media is bright, add a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity; if it is already dark, or AVKit playback controls already dim, do not add one. Appearance also changes when people pick a Liquid Glass look or turn on Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast. — [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)
- iOS and iPadOS still have four **content-layer** materials: ultraThin, thin, regular (default), thick. Thicker is more opaque and better for fine text; thinner keeps more background context. Vibrant label/fill/separator colors are required on materials. Avoid quaternary labels on thin and ultraThin because contrast is too low. Do not pick a material for the color it appears to add; system settings change that color. — [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)
- Tab bars changelog on the live page: 28 July 2025 “Added guidance for Liquid Glass”; 16 December 2025 “Updated guidance for Liquid Glass”; 8 June 2026 “Updated terminology and art.” No earlier 2024 row was in that extract. — [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)
- Structural tab-bar rules that remain, and are not a new skin: a tab bar is for navigating sections, not for actions (use a toolbar for actions on the current view). Keep it visible except when a modal covers it. Do not disable or hide tab buttons. Avoid the overflow “More” tab. Include short labels. Prefer filled SF Symbols. Badges only for critical information. Do not tint tab labels in a color close to a bright content background. — [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)
- iOS tab bar **container** change: it “floats above content at the bottom.” Items sit on Liquid Glass so content peeks through. With an accessory (Music’s MiniPlayer), the bar may minimize on scroll down and the accessory moves inline; leave minimized state by tapping a tab or scrolling to the top (`TabBarMinimizeBehavior`). A dedicated search tab may sit at the trailing end. Compact (portrait) stacks icon over label; regular (landscape) places icon and label side by side. — [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)
- iPadOS tab bar placement is a layout difference, not only a material: the system shows it near the **top**. It can stay a tab bar or convert to a sidebar (`tabBarOnly` / `sidebarAdaptable`). People may customize which sections are in the bar; default to five or fewer so compact and regular sizes stay continuous. — [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)
- Toolbars page changelog: 9 June 2025 added grouping guidance, updated symbol guidance, and **incorporated navigation-bar guidance into Toolbars**. 16 December 2025 updated Liquid Glass guidance. “In iOS, a navigation-specific toolbar is sometimes called a navigation bar.” — [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- Toolbar structure that is specified, not merely painted: items group on the leading edge (back, sidebar, title, document menu — not customizable), center (common controls; collapses to the system overflow as the window shrinks), and trailing edge (inspectors, search, More, and the single primary action). Navigation and critical actions (Done, Close, Save) stay in their own groups. Aim for at most three groups. One `.prominent` action, trailing. Text-labeled actions must not sit in the same cluster as symbol actions. Symbols should not wear their own circular borders; the glass group is the container. Custom backgrounds and tinted controls are discouraged because they fight system background effects; use a scroll-edge effect instead. Corner radii of custom items should be concentric with the bar. — [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- Large titles are still specified, not retired: “Use a large title to help people stay oriented as they navigate and scroll. By default, a large title transitions to a standard title as people begin scrolling the content, and transitions back to large when people scroll to the top.” Developer hook remains `prefersLargeTitles`. Titles should be a word or short phrase, under 15 characters, and must not be the app name. — [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars); type spec at default Large Dynamic Type: Large Title is Regular 34 pt / 41 pt leading, emphasized weight Bold — [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- iPadOS can put a toolbar and a tab bar in the **same top horizontal band**, so the window width stays available for content. That is a layout combination, not a new widget dashboard. — [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)
- Layout page: “Differentiate controls from content” by using Liquid Glass on controls and a scroll-edge effect instead of a solid or semi-opaque bar fill. Full-screen backgrounds should extend under sidebars, toolbars, and tab bars. If a sidebar would crop an important part of a background image, a background-extension effect flips and blurs the image under the component. — [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- Safe areas, current wording: a safe area is the part of a window not covered at the edge by hardware or by another view “like a toolbar, tab bar, or status bar.” Respecting it is required so system UI and the Dynamic Island do not cover content and controls. Also respect layout margins and guides. Decide layout from size classes (compact/regular), not from device idiom or portrait/landscape. Do not change functionality when size class changes; a larger size may swap a tab bar for a sidebar or reveal overflow. Dynamic Type may force horizontal neighbors to stack and single-line rows to grow. — [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- Sheets are still a scoped task attached to the parent, not a new navigation model. iOS/iPadOS sheets may be modal or nonmodal (Notes’ format sheet stays up while editing). One sheet at a time from the main interface. iPhone resizable sheets use detents: system **large** (fully expanded) and **medium** (about half); a grabber is required on a resizable sheet and is a VoiceOver affordance; swipe-to-dismiss is expected, with an action sheet if there are unsaved changes. Medium detent is for progressive disclosure (share sheet); compose flows such as Messages and Mail stay full height. iPadOS should prefer page or form sheet styles. Changelog: 29 March 2024 added the iPadOS page/form-sheet guidance; 24 March 2026 “Updated guidance for button placement” (Cancel leading, Done trailing; do not show Cancel, Done, and Back together). The sheets page fetched does **not** say the sheet surface itself is Liquid Glass. — [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)
- iPhone Duo HIG, new page 9 September 2026, is a structural change of **where the same controls sit**. Outer display: toolbars and tab bars move to the side to keep vertical space, and stay on the side when the inner display is landscape. Inner portrait keeps horizontal bars. Controls on that vertical axis, top to bottom: Dynamic Island, status bar, toolbar, tab bar. Top of the axis is for Back/Close, then prominent actions such as Done. Content area is asymmetrical; use safe areas. Additional reserved regions: outer camera (always, expands to Dynamic Island), inner camera (only while active), and the fold when partially open. Alerts, menus, and sheets move off the fold; split views change column width. New container: an arrangement view (split or overlay) for two content panes. Do not put navigation inside it. When space is tight, either collapse toolbar items into overflow and keep the tab bar (default, navigation-focused) or minimize the tab bar and keep the toolbar (task-focused). Do not override default bar placement. Text-labeled buttons stay horizontal, so prefer symbols. Mail on the closed device is one pane; open, list and message sit side by side — same functions, extra hierarchy level, not a new app. — [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)
- Mandatory hit size, buttons page (changelog 9 June 2025 and 16 December 2025 for Liquid Glass, not for the size rule): “a button needs a hit region of at least 44x44 pt” (visionOS 60x60). One or two prominent buttons per view. Distinguish the preferred action by style, not by a different size. — [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)
- Accessibility page (expanded 7 March 2025; Assistive Access / Nutrition Labels added 9 June 2025) is stricter and more nuanced than a single 44 pt slogan. iOS/iPadOS **default** control size 44×44 pt, **minimum** control size 28×28 pt. About 12 pt padding around bezeled controls; about 24 pt around unbezeled visible edges. Text: default 17 pt, minimum 11 pt on iOS/iPadOS. Contrast, using WCAG AA as the values Accessibility Inspector checks: up to 17 pt, any weight, 4.5:1; 18 pt, 3:1; any bold text, 3:1. If the default theme misses that, provide a higher-contrast scheme when Increase Contrast is on, and check both light and dark. Do not encode state by color alone. — [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility); sizes repeated on [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Dynamic Type is mandatory where the platform has it (iOS, iPadOS, and others; macOS does not). Layout must adapt at every size, including Larger Accessibility sizes. Meaningful icons scale with the text. Avoid truncating scrollable text unless another view shows the rest. At large sizes, stack inline metadata under the primary line and reduce column count. Do not scale everything equally: tab titles need not grow just because body text does, but hierarchy (primary content toward the top) must stay. Typography specs now include an emphasized weight per style (page text includes this table; a third-party HIG mirror dated the emphasized-weight addition 16 December 2025 — that date was **not** on the typography changelog in the fetch, so treat the December date as unverified). Default Large body is 17 pt Regular / 22 leading; AX1 body is 28 pt. Avoid Ultralight, Thin, and Light, especially at small sizes. — [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Reduce Motion, current accessibility page: when the setting is on, reduce automatic and repetitive animation, including zoom, scale, and peripheral motion. Specifically: tighten springs to cut bounce; track animations to the gesture; avoid depth changes on the z-axis; replace x/y/z moves with fades; avoid animating into and out of blurs. Also avoid auto-dismiss timers and autoplay without a stop control. — [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- HIG home extract lists three principles tied to this design: Hierarchy (controls elevate and distinguish content), Harmony (concentric with the hardware), Consistency (platform conventions across window sizes). Developer guidance pointer is “Adopting Liquid Glass.” — [Human Interface Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/)

### Inferences
- The 2025 change is a **layer split** (controls float; content does not become glass) plus scroll-linked shrinking of a bar that was already at the bottom. A theme can copy the layer split — persistent navigation separated from content, one primary action, search as a destination — without copying refraction, blur, or specular highlights.
- Large titles, collapsing on scroll, sheet detents, and safe areas are continuity, not a 2025 redesign. Restyling those with glass does not count as a new layout.
- iPhone Duo is the 2026 structural exception: the same tab bar and toolbar change axis. A phone layout that assumes “chrome is always a bottom band” is no longer true for that device. For a non-Duo product, the portable rule is: keep navigation and actions in system bars, and let the bar’s edge follow the safe area rather than hard-coding the bottom.
- Liquid Glass is a poor foundation for a brand theme. Apple forbids it on content, requires regular glass (or a 35% dim) wherever text sits on it, and tells Reduce Motion users to avoid blur animations and bouncy springs. The material also changes under Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast, so a look that only works in the default translucent state will fail an accessibility setting.

The only 2024 sheets changelog line found is the 29 March 2024 iPad page/form-sheet note. Claims that iOS 18 itself moved tab bars, killed large titles, or changed safe areas are unverified here.

## What in Material 3 Expressive is a structural idea, and what is a skin?

### Takeaway
M3 Expressive (13 May 2025, Google I/O; not a new “M4”) does change navigation **containers**: a shorter flexible navigation bar, an expanded rail that replaces the drawer, and a docked-versus-floating toolbar that must not sit with the nav bar. Shape morph, 35 decorative shapes, emphasized type, and spring motion are emphasis tools on those containers. They do not, by themselves, change what the screen is.

### Cited Findings
- Material Design’s own post, 13 May 2025: M3 Expressive is “an evolution of the Material 3 design system,” explicitly “not a new version,” not deprecating M3, and not “M4.” It adds components, motion physics, color, and “design tactics.” Google says this is its most researched update since 2014: 46 studies, more than 18,000 participants. Google’s reported outcomes: expressive designs preferred across ages; higher scores for playfulness, energy, creativity, friendliness; people more likely to switch to products using the components; participants spotted key elements up to four times faster. Those outcomes are Google’s claims, linked to a design.google research article, not an independent replication. — [Start building with Material 3 Expressive](https://m3.material.io/blog/building-with-m3-expressive)
- Fourteen new or updated components get more configuration, shape options, and emphasized text. Seven tactics: (1) mix shapes, (2) use color contrast across roles to prioritize actions, (3) emphasized type for headlines and actions, (4) group content in containers and give the main task the brightest surface, (5) shape morph and springs, (6) move controls when the window changes, including foldables and canonical layouts, (7) at most one or two “hero moments.” Caution on the same page: smaller shapes can make an essential action look less important; without color contrast, elements blend; ungrouped information blends. — [Start building with Material 3 Expressive](https://m3.material.io/blog/building-with-m3-expressive)
- Shape page, “M3 Expressive update, May 2025”: 35 new shapes and shape morphing in the Figma shape library and Jetpack Compose; corner-radius tokens Large increased to 20 dp, Extra large increased to 32 dp, Extra extra large 48 dp; fully rounded corners use the token `full` instead of 50% of the component. Rectangular shapes are fully rounded by default; individual corners can be asymmetric. Shape morph is for state (selected), progress (typing, loading), or environment (sound, temperature, time) — a communication change, not a new page structure. “Shape is versatile, not semantic”: do not assign one meaning to one shape. “Use abstract shapes sparingly”; shapes without a reason “add more visual clutter than delight.” Decorative use (crops, avatars) is the flexible case. — [Shape](https://m3.material.io/styles/shape/overview)
- Typography, per the May 2025 post: emphasized styles for variable and static fonts, meant to reinforce hierarchy and “bold editorial layouts,” for example a “begin recording” action or unread messages. This is weight and scale inside the existing type ramp, not a new navigation skeleton. — [Start building with Material 3 Expressive](https://m3.material.io/blog/building-with-m3-expressive)
- Navigation bar, “M3 Expressive update, May 2025”: a **flexible navigation bar replaces the original**, which is deprecated. It is shorter and can show horizontal navigation items in medium windows. Active label color changes from on-surface-variant to secondary (a color-role change, not a layout change). Historical contrast still on that page: M2 used a drop shadow; baseline M3 is taller, shadowless, with a filled icon and an active indicator. — [Navigation bar](https://m3.material.io/components/navigation-bar)
- Navigation rail, May 2025: collapsed and expanded rails replace the original rail. The expanded rail is meant to **replace the navigation drawer**. Expanded modality can be non-modal. Active state remains a pill indicator (that pill is M3, not new in 2025). — [Navigation rail](http://m3.material.io/components/navigation-rail/overview)
- App bars, May 2025: component renamed from top app bar to app bar. New search app bar (icons inside and outside, centered text, opens search view). Medium and large app bars are being deprecated in favor of medium flexible and large flexible; the small app bar gets the same flexible behavior. On scroll, separation is a fill color, not a drop shadow (that shadow removal is the M2→M3 difference, still stated). — [Top app bar](https://m3.material.io/components/top-app-bar/overview)
- Toolbars (component page reached via bottom-app-bar guidelines URL): two types. **Docked** spans the full window width and is for global actions that stay constant across pages. **Floating** sits above body content and is for contextual actions. Do not show a navigation bar and a toolbar at the same time, because both occupy the bottom. Pattern: navigation bar on primary pages, toolbar on later pages that have actions. A floating toolbar that also contains navigation, stacked on a nav bar, is explicitly “Don’t.” — [Toolbar guidelines](https://m3.material.io/components/bottom-app-bar/guidelines)
- Google Design, 22 May 2025, names five new expressive components: button groups (built-in shape morph), FAB menu (several actions), loading indicator, split button (related actions in less space), toolbars that can be floating or docked. — [Google Design on X](https://x.com/GoogleDesign/status/1925627723034071098)
- Menus, “M3 Expressive update, November 2025”: vertical menus recommended for new work, with new shapes, color styles, selection states, and submenu motion. Gaps are an Android layout option for grouping actions, as an alternative to full-width dividers. Baseline menus remain available. A 9to5Google report (22 November 2025) adds that corners are rounder and the selection no longer spans the full container width; that extra visual detail is secondary. — [Menus](https://m3.material.io/components/menus); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/11/22/material-3-expressive-menus/)
- Motion: the May 2025 post defines two spring types. Spatial springs (position, rotation, size, corner radius) overshoot and settle. Effects springs (color, opacity) do not overshoot. The easing-and-duration page now says components and motion use the physics system, products should migrate, and the easing/duration system “is still used for transitions” but “is no longer maintained.” Older M3 easing pairs (for example emphasized 500 ms begin-and-end) remain documented as the unmaintained path. — [Motion: how it works](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/overview/how-it-works); [Easing and duration](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/easing-and-duration)
- Color system: roles are paired so a dark surface gets a light label and contrast is built in; three user-selectable contrast levels (standard, medium, high); dynamic color and a built-in dark theme. A Google codelab states the WCAG targets the system is aiming at: normal text AA 4.5:1 (AAA 7:1), large text AA 3:1 (AAA 4.5:1), non-text graphics AA 3:1. The codelab’s own “last updated” line says 05/11/22, so the ratio table is older than Expressive; the three-level contrast control is described on the current color page. — [Color](https://m3.material.io/styles/color); [Codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/color-contrast-accessibility)
- Touch size still in force on an Expressive component: each button in a button group needs a minimum 48×48 dp target; extra-small and small groups keep padding so the target is larger than the visible control. The archived Material usability page (Material 1, not maintained) is the source of the often-quoted 48×48 dp ≈ 9 mm target and 8 dp spacing. Treat 48 dp as confirmed for M3 Expressive button groups; treat the 8 dp / 9 mm explanation as the older archived guideline, not as a 2025 rewrite. — [Button groups accessibility](https://m3.material.io/components/button-groups/accessibility); [Archived accessibility](https://material.io/archive/guidelines/usability/accessibility.html)
- TechCrunch, 13 May 2025, quotes Mindy Brooks (VP Product and UX, Android Platform): Expressive is an expansion of Material You meant to add emotion and make key elements easier to use. The article also says the update “subtly blurs the background” for depth, and describes springy motion plus haptics when dismissing a notification. Blur-for-depth is a skin. The quote is a press briefing, not a spec. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/13/google-unveils-its-new-android-design-language-material-3-expressive/)

### Inferences
- Structural ideas worth keeping even if the skin is rejected: one bottom navigation container on primary screens (shorter bar **or** a contextual toolbar, not both); an expanded rail instead of a drawer when the window grows; flexible app bars that change height with scroll instead of a permanent large header plus a shadow; search as its own expanding surface; gaps that group actions without a full-bleed divider.
- Shape morph, 35-shape decoration, extra corner radius, emphasized type, vibrant color, and spatial overshoot are skins and motion styles. They can sit on an unchanged screen. Google’s own cautions say decoration that shrinks the primary action, or shapes with no job, makes the UI worse.
- “Up to four times faster” is not a layout measurement this research reproduced. Do not treat it as proof that rounder corners or blur improve a financial product.

### Gaps
- Exact dp height of the “shorter” flexible navigation bar versus the old bar was not stated in the fetched text (a third-party React port calls the legacy bar 80 dp; that port is not the spec).
- No Material.io page through October 2026 was found that replaces the May 2025 navigation model with another one. November 2025 menus are an addition, not a new app skeleton. A full 2026 Expressive changelog was not found.
- Android’s “honor reduced motion” sentence was not quoted from material.io. Apple’s Reduce Motion rules are the sourced motion-accessibility requirements in this note. The local design database (below) also flags reduced motion, but that is not a Google spec.
- Compose/MDC web support is uneven (navigation bar and app bar pages mark Web: Expressive as unavailable). Do not assume the expressive nav bar exists as a web component on material.io.

## Which OEM skins and premium utility apps show a real layout move?

### Takeaway
Nothing OS 5.0’s sourced layout move is a split notification shade, not a new app grid; most of its 2026 marketing is type, translucency, and clock faces. Among the requested apps, only Linear’s 2025 mobile navigation rebuild and Craft 3’s 2024 information architecture are verified structural moves. Things’ 2026 store notes are a glass restyle. Cash App, Arc Search, Apple Invites, and Teenage Engineering had no verified 2024–2026 layout source in this pass. Apple Journal’s later structure is reported by press, not by an Apple page fetched here.

## What do these systems refuse, what accessibility risks follow, and what did the local design database return?

### Takeaway
Both platforms refuse a decorated content layer that pretends to be navigation: Apple forbids Liquid Glass behind content and forbids actions in the tab bar; Material forbids stacking a nav bar with a toolbar and forbids shapes that do not help. The shared risks are contrast on translucency, type below the body default, and motion that bounces or blurs. The local style database’s “premium editorial” recommendation came back as Liquid Glass — a corpus echo, not a live product, and not a theme to adopt.

### Cited Findings
- Apple refusals that are layout rules, not taste: no Liquid Glass in the content layer; use it sparingly even on custom controls; clear glass only over media, with a 35% dark dim if the media is bright; no quaternary vibrancy on thin/ultraThin; tab bar is not an action bar; do not hide or disable tabs; avoid More-tab overflow; do not color tab or toolbar labels like the content behind them; do not paint custom toolbar backgrounds; one prominent button role per decision, and not on a destructive action; toolbar groups capped around three; titles under 15 characters and not the app name; one sheet at a time; sheets need a non-Done exit; Duo bars stay on the system edge. — [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials); [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars); [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars); [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons); [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets); [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)
- Material refusals: do not show a navigation bar and a toolbar together; do not give a shape a fixed semantic meaning; do not use abstract shapes that lack a reason; do not shrink the shape of an essential action until it looks unimportant; do not rely on color without contrast; baseline menus remain available but are not the expressive recommendation. M3 Expressive does not retire M3. — [Toolbar guidelines](https://m3.material.io/components/bottom-app-bar/guidelines); [Shape](https://m3.material.io/styles/shape/overview); [Expressive overview](https://m3.material.io/blog/building-with-m3-expressive)
- Contrast risk on glass: Apple shows a non-vibrant `systemGray3` label on a material as a failure, and a vibrant label as the fix. Regular glass exists specifically because background content can make text illegible; clear glass without the 35% dim is for dark or already-dimmed media only. Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency change the material, so a screenshot of default glass is not the accessible state. Inspector thresholds: 4.5:1 up to 17 pt, 3:1 at 18 pt or for bold. Check light and dark. — [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials); [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Small-type risk: iOS minimum 11 pt, default 17 pt, but the usable requirement is Dynamic Type through accessibility sizes (body 28 pt at AX1 on the published table) with reflow, not a scaled screenshot. Light weights fail first. Tab titles are allowed to stay small when body text grows; body text is not. — [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography); [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
- Motion risk: Reduce Motion means less bounce (tighten springs), no blur transitions, fades instead of axis moves. That collides with Liquid Glass refraction and with M3 spatial springs, which are defined to overshoot. Effects springs (opacity, color) are the less risky motion. Apple also says flashing and autoplay need an off switch. — [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility); [M3 motion](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/overview/how-it-works)
- Target-size risk: Apple hit region “at least 44×44 pt,” with an accessibility table that still lists 28×28 pt as the iOS minimum control size and asks for ~12 pt (bezel) or ~24 pt (no bezel) padding. Material button groups require 48×48 dp targets even when the visible control is smaller. These numbers are not interchangeable. A control that is 44 CSS pixels is not automatically 44 pt or 48 dp. — [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons); [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility); [Button groups accessibility](https://m3.material.io/components/button-groups/accessibility)

### Inferences
- A layout that survives both systems is: content on an opaque or standard surface; one navigation container; actions grouped and visually separate from navigation; type that can reflow; targets at the platform minimum with space between them; motion that can become a fade. Frost, gradient meshes, neon, and extra floating cards fail the “don’t decorate the content layer” rule even when they are fashionable.
- The 28×28 pt “minimum” must not be used to undercut the 44×44 pt hit region for a primary control. Read 28 pt as Apple’s floor for a control that still has padding around it, and 44 pt as the hit region buttons are told to meet.

### Gaps
- No measured contrast ratio for default Liquid Glass over photographic content was published on the pages read. The risk is stated qualitatively (illegibility, 35% dim, vibrant colors), not as a lab number.
- Material’s reduced-motion token behavior was not quoted from material.io.
