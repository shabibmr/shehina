# Lock a paper column on phones

## 1. A short map of the field

Between 2024 and 31 October 2026, the structural change in mobile layout is a fixed frame — a mode bar, a masthead, a column, or a map — with one zone allowed to change its job, not a new decoration on a stack of cards. The New York Times, from October 2024, keeps the bottom bar as modes and swipes sideways between front pages, while The Atlantic, in frames posted on 27 October 2026, keeps one masthead and a four-item bar and lets that screen become a full-bleed story, an author column, a games shelf, or an audio stage ([NYT Co.](https://www.nytco.com/press/our-new-times-app/), [Thompson](https://x.com/nxthompson/status/2115220789977067642)). Apple and Google contradict each other on the control layer: the 9 June 2025 Apple announcement pulls the tab bar back as you scroll so content can take the screen, and Material 3 Expressive, in May 2025, makes the primary action larger and moves it into the thumb zone, as with Send sitting just above the keyboard ([Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-introduces-a-delightful-and-elegant-new-software-design/), [Google research](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Liquid Glass, introduced in that same Apple announcement, is a material for controls and navigation, not a layout, and Apple’s materials guidance forbids it in the content layer; a local style-catalog match of that glass plus a gold accent is a database echo, not a product ([Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). The other documented structures are narrower job changes on one screen — words pinned under a picture, two labeled verbs on a product, a map as the canvas, a wait used as a reading slot, a launch instead of a catalog, a tool that appears only after the previous step — and one 2026 placement change, iPhone Duo, whose guidance of 9 September 2026 moves those bars to the side ([iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)).

## 2. Twelve directions

Order is distinctive and shippable: a person can read it, a flow can stay one system, and ordinary mobile UI can build it. Nothing here scores a 5. The opened products already show the failures: clipped type, a ribbon people must learn, a player that stacks into a dashboard, a listen control that moves, dense pins, an 11px price, six tabs, a collage that can date, shape clutter, and glass that loses contrast.

### Paper masthead, with color kept off the reading path

1. **Name.** Paper masthead, with color kept off the reading path.

2. **References.** The Atlantic app, frames posted by CEO Nicholas Thompson on 27 October 2026, with the Play listing’s “what’s new” dated 23 October 2026 ([thread](https://x.com/nxthompson/status/2115220789977067642), [home](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrEfMhbIAACBUk.jpg), [author](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrEjgLakAAIx-e.jpg), [games](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrEpspbcAAUeIF.jpg), [audio](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVrFERAasAAGRdO.jpg), [Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.theatlantic.newsstand&hl=en_US)). Cousin, not the same screen: Blue Bottle’s Morning Notes, the editorial slot in the wait between order and pickup, Work & Co, 9 September 2025 ([Work & Co](https://www.work.co/news/blue-bottle-launches-new-mobile-app/)). Morning Notes is the “a non-catalog surface may read” idea inside a coffee app. It is not a news masthead.

3. **Layout.** One masthead and one column stay put. The first region may change job: a full-bleed photograph and one story, a centered author column, a stack of colored panels, or an audio stage that replaces the tab bar. What stays fixed in the opened Atlantic frames is a white ground, a centered serif screen title, and a bottom bar labeled Home, Latest, Audio, and Games. What may change is the body under that title. The lead story has no card edge; the photograph is the screen width. Thompson also says Home has carousels and more story sizes; the opened Home frame does not show a carousel, so that part is his claim, not a seen layout. Blue Bottle’s fixed piece is different: three anchors, and the wait is the surface that changes. Do not merge those into one navigation diagram.

4. **Style.** In the Atlantic frames the base is white, the type is black, and red is the only functional color on the reading screens (active Home, back chevron, Follow, scrubber). The lead photo runs edge to edge with no radius and no stroke. A small credit sits on the photo. The headline and deck are serif; the byline is small, centered, and widely spaced. Icons at the masthead are simple: a person at the left, search at the right. Games breaks the paper on purpose: rounded panels in pink, orange, and yellow, with black drawings. The audio sheet uses four flat fields (coral, mustard, olive, brown), not a blurred cover, then a white column, a red scrubber, and black transport icons. The system refuses extra hues on Home and Author, a tinted or blurred photo behind the headline, and a second accent. The serif’s family name is not specified in the sources. Corner radii in numbers are not specified; the lead has none, and the games panels are rounded. The bar looks rounded in the frames, and it looks translucent; calling that Liquid Glass would be an inference, not a label in the posts. Blue Bottle’s stated palette is a signature blue, warm tones instead of the old stark white, and rich photography, including one Japanese woodblock on the wait surface. Type family, sizes, radii, and motion are not specified in those posts. They refused a literal copy of the packaging because digital type and color had to stay legible ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/inside-reimagined-blue-bottle-coffee-mobile-app/)). An unofficial extraction of aesop.com, updated 13 May 2026, is a paper catalog (cream ground, graphite hairline, square corners, one serif headline) but it is not an Aesop brand book and not a confirmed native app; its 11px prices are a failure, recorded in the risks, not a token to adopt ([Aesop extraction](https://www.shadcn.io/design/aesop)).

5. **Navigation and density.** Four modes sit in one bar over the content. Home is sparse on purpose: one photograph, one story, and only the top of the next story. Games is the dense screen. The author screen keeps the calm column and adds a mini player over the photograph, so two bottom layers want the same place. The full audio sheet removes the tab bar and puts transport, a queue, and two black buttons in its place. The public iOS help still sends people to a Search tab for sections and Magazine, which is not the bar in the frames ([Atlantic Help](https://support.theatlantic.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038751393-The-Atlantic-s-iOS-App)). Safe-area insets are not specified in the sources. The practical evidence is overlap.

6. **Screen rhythm.** Home: masthead, one full-bleed photo, one story block, the start of a second story, mode bar. Author, the detail-like screen: no wordmark, a red back chevron, a centered portrait and name, a rule and LATEST, then a full-width photo, plus the mini player. Games: the same title position, then colored posters and date chips instead of prose. Audio: a sheet, not a tab, with a way back to the story. Latest is a tab and was not opened. Blue Bottle’s equivalent of a secondary screen is the wait, not a second storefront. Its screen order was not available as text.

7. **Risks.** The opened frames already fail in small, specific ways. The lead photo takes most of the first screen, so the next story is only a headline and a thumbnail. Date chips run off a games panel. “Suspicious Incident” sits under the tab bar. The audio title is cut. The author photo sits partly behind the mini player. The red scrubber on white is thin. Games’ three hues are a different standard from the article pages, and they are more than one accent. Whether the clips are the real layout or the crop of a marketing frame cannot be proven; they are the frames the CEO posted, with a 9:41 status bar. No contrast ratio or hit size was published. Copying the translucent bar would take on the glass-contrast problem Apple states for text on translucent materials ([Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). The Aesop extraction, same caveat as above, sets running text at 14px and prices and captions at 11px. That is the paper-catalog habit this direction has to refuse. Apple’s own minimum text size is 11 pt; that floor is still too small for a price or a caption someone must read.

8. **Scores.** Originality 4, readability 3, consistency 3, buildability 4. The sequence of masthead, photo, and column is uncommon next to equal cards and ordinary to build, but the frames already cover type, and Games plus a second bottom layer break the paper system.

### Sideways front pages on a fixed mode bar

1. **Name.** Sideways front pages on a fixed mode bar.

2. **References.** The New York Times app, October 2024, still the design discussed in June 2025 ([NYT Co.](https://www.nytco.com/press/our-new-times-app/), [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91199198/the-new-york-times-app-redesign), [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/10/the-new-york-times-redesigns-its-app-to-highlight-a-universe-beyond-just-news/), [WAN-IFRA](https://wan-ifra.org/2025/06/read-play-swipe-the-strategy-behind-the-new-york-times-app-revamp/)). Second reference, for the bar only, not for the ribbon: Letterboxd Android 3.0.0, 27 August 2025 ([Letterboxd](https://letterboxd.com/crew/story/huge-update-to-our-android-app-is-out-now/)). Letterboxd does not document sideways homes. It documents where the main action sits in the bar, and what leaves the detail screen.

3. **Layout.** You still open on Today. The bottom bar is how you read, hear, play, or personalize. Nieman Lab and WAN-IFRA name the items Home, Listen, Play, and You. The top ribbon is a row of other front pages: the press post puts Election 2024, Lifestyle, Great Reads, and Opinion one way, and Games, Cooking, and The Athletic the other. Nieman also names Wirecutter on the product side. Lifestyle may be more photographic than text-heavy Today. Those product sections are previews that hand off to other apps, not full copies of Cooking or The Athletic. What may not change: Today remains the center, and the bar does not become a super-app launcher. Letterboxd’s move is narrower. The side drawer is gone. Log and review sit in the center of the bottom bar, where a floating button used to be. On the film screen, cast and crew scroll vertically, and reviews leave for one gray tile under the ratings chart and the watch rows. Settings sit behind a gear on Profile.

4. **Style.** Not specified in the sources for the Times: palette, radius, icon weight, or type family. Fast Company says core type and spacing were left alone in the 2024 change. Lifestyle is “visual” because the photography earns it; looping video was a plan in June 2025, not a shown result. Letterboxd’s crew post specifies “the gray tile” and nothing else. No poster grid, green, or type claim is in that post. Do not invent them.

5. **Navigation and density.** Two axes, and they must stay different jobs. The bar is the mode. The ribbon is which front page. Pugpig, quoting the team, says signature journalism is to one side and the product portfolio to the other, with puzzles and opinion far out so people travel ([Pugpig](https://www.pugpig.com/2025/05/16/how-the-new-york-times-updated-app-supports-its-subscription-bundle-business/)). Play in the bar and Puzzles in the ribbon open the same games as different modes. You exists because an older For You mix did not explain itself. A column read more than three or five times can be added to You. They count an engaged click as at least 30 seconds, not a raw tap. Letterboxd’s density move is the opposite of a ribbon: fewer things on the film screen, and the primary action in the thumb’s center. A second tap on Profile or Search resets that tab. Tablet support is not in that Android update. Safe areas are not specified in the sources.

6. **Screen rhythm.** Home is Today, text-led. The next swipe, Lifestyle, is the picture front. Great Reads and Opinion are further homes, not filters of Today. The article page was not described in the launch posts. Listen, Play, and You are different jobs, and games inside the news app are a preview. Letterboxd’s film screen is the detail: ratings and watch rows stay, reviews are one step away, cast is its own vertical scroll. Profile is the secondary screen and owns Settings. Home and the diary were not in the post. iOS structure was not opened; the Android post says it is chasing iOS, which implies a bottom bar already existed there, but that layout is not quoted.

7. **Risks.** The ribbon only works if people learn it. The redesign was meant to look familiar, which lowers shock and also means the new map can be missed. Popular sections at the far ends help only after someone knows to swipe. Two doors to the same games can read as a duplicate. The stated fear is abundance and an unexplained personal front, not tiny body type. Letterboxd trades a floating button that covered the poster for an extra tap to reach reviews, and the post warns that almost every screen changed and bugs are likely. No contrast ratio or hit size was published for either app.

8. **Scores.** Originality 4, readability 4, consistency 3, buildability 4. Type was deliberately left alone, so reading is not the failure; the second map is easy to miss and easy to duplicate, which is why consistency stays at 3.

### A column that may bleed only by license

1. **Name.** A column that may bleed only by license.

2. **References.** Apple News Format, the article system inside News, not the Today tab. The pages are the layout object, planning, positioning, and tutorials ([Layout](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenewsformat/layout), [Planning](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/planning-the-layout-for-your-article), [Positioning](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/positioning-the-content-in-your-article), [Tutorials](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/applenews/apple-news-format-tutorials)). A direct fetch of the planning article returned only the title in that pass, so those figures are from the indexed page text, not a second full read. This is one format, not two products. The Atlantic home is a one-column cousin; nothing opened shows that it uses this grid. No second consumer app was verified on these rules. Trade press about the News app’s own tabs (Sports, Following, five bottom items) is not this direction and is not an Apple page ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/11/04/ios-26-2s-apple-news-app-has-a-new-and-improved-design/), [Stuff](https://www.stuff.tv/features/apple-news-how-use-it/)).

3. **Layout.** Components sit on one column grid. The indexed docs say the default is 7 columns, designed width 1024 pt, margin 60, gutter 20. On a wider screen News adds margin rather than more columns, and the gutter stays fixed. Apple says 7 columns are enough to scale to iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro; under 5 columns there may not be enough information to keep the design when the screen shrinks. What may change, only as a named component: ignore the viewport padding and run full-bleed, or ignore the document margin and enter the designed margin without growing on a Mac. A header may be layered and then covered by the body. A caption may sit in the wide right margin. Body text may wrap an inset pull quote or an inset photo. Roles stay named: title, body, caption, quote, image. On a phone, the usable reading of this rule is one text column, with a bleed or an overlap only where that component is allowed. It is not an instruction to draw seven columns on a narrow screen.

4. **Style.** No brand palette for the News app chrome is in these pages. The layered-header tutorial is about setting title colors so type stays legible on an image. Full-bleed is opt-in, so the default is inset. Type family, icon weight, and corner radius are not specified in the sources. The system refuses an unaligned collage and, on very wide screens, refuses letting images grow without a limit.

5. **Navigation and density.** This direction is the article, not the app’s tab bar. Apple’s current iPhone guide, separate from the format, describes Today as the editor-and-Siri feed and Following as the place for channels, topics, and a smaller Favorites set ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/iphone/iph5b557ed3d/ios)). An older copy of a similar guide is explicitly iOS 12 and is historical, not the 2026 screen ([iOS 12 path](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/iphone/iph5b557ed3d/12.0/ios/12.0)). Stuff’s June 2026 count of five bottom items is a magazine’s account, not Apple’s. Density inside the article comes from the grid, not from a second chrome.

6. **Screen rhythm.** There is no home in this format. The article opens on a header that can be image-led, then a column of body, with an optional side caption and an inset quote. Today versus Following, in the support guide, is a feed versus a management screen, not a visual comparison. A story page for the Times or The Atlantic was not opened, so this rhythm should not be pasted onto those apps from guesswork.

7. **Risks.** Fewer than five columns may not survive the shrink to a phone. Type on a photograph needs a legibility color, which the tutorial treats as part of the header, not as an afterthought. Parallax means the body overlaps the header; if the header still contains type, that overlap is a collision. A side caption uses margin that tightens or disappears on a phone. The docs say the grid’s job is to reflow that. They do not say every side caption stays beside the text on iPhone. No contrast ratio was given beyond the legibility instruction.

8. **Scores.** Originality 3, readability 4, consistency 4, buildability 4. The license is the useful part, and it is buildable, but it is a publisher grammar rather than a whole-app theme, and the phone will not show the wide-screen caption as drawn.

### Three anchors, and the wait is a reading slot

1. **Name.** Three anchors, and the wait is a reading slot.

2. **References.** Blue Bottle Coffee’s iOS and Android app, Work & Co (Accenture Song), news post 9 September 2025, and a design-director interview on 3 November 2025 ([Work & Co](https://www.work.co/news/blue-bottle-launches-new-mobile-app/), [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/inside-reimagined-blue-bottle-coffee-mobile-app/)). System cousin, not a coffee app: Material 3 Expressive’s email study, May 2025, which moves Send out of the top bar and makes it a larger button just above the keyboard, in a secondary color ([Google research](https://design.google/library/expressive-material-design-google-research)). Constraint example only: Metalab’s Atoms app allows three habits at a time, and a new one stays locked until there is progress. The case study states no year, so it is not evidence from 2024–2026 ([Atoms](https://www.metalab.com/work/atoms)).

3. **Layout.** Blue Bottle, in the client’s words, trades typical navigation for three points: café location, order, and the logo. The second move is temporal. The gap between order and pickup is not a spinner. It is Morning Notes: art and the story of the piece, neighborhood spotlights, brand and brewing notes, lyrics. What stays fixed is the three-target frame and a saved path to pickup. What changes is the note in the wait. The Material cousin is the same idea at the scale of one control: the primary verb leaves the top cluster and sits where the thumb already is, larger than the secondary tools. Atoms is the negative space around that idea. A hard slot count needs an escape. The page specifies snooze and frequency changes so a missed day does not break the layout. Do not cite Atoms as a dated ship.

4. **Style.** Blue Bottle states a signature blue, warm tones instead of stark white, rich photography, and “East and West” influences, with the woodblock as the one published example. “Minimal yet tactile” is a mood in the interview, not a token. Type, size, radius, stroke, and icon weight are not specified in the sources. The screens were not available as text, and the blue is not given as a hex. The refusal is the style rule that was actually written: do not transfer packaging type and color if they fail legibility. Material’s style levers, if you import the cousin, are a larger size, a secondary color, rounder corners (large 20 dp, extra large 32 dp, extra extra large 48 dp, and a `full` token), 35 shapes, and springs. Shape “is versatile, not semantic”: do not assign a function to one shape, and use abstract shapes sparingly ([Shape](https://m3.material.io/styles/shape/overview-principles)). The expressive motion scheme overshoots and is for hero moments; a standard scheme barely bounces and is for utilitarian products. That motion wording is from an indexed extract of the motion page in one pass, not a full re-read ([Motion](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion/overview/how-it-works)). Google Sans Flex is the type named on the shape page; no other ramp was stated there.

5. **Navigation and density.** Three targets, not five. The order of the three controls is not specified in the sources. The wait is one editorial surface, not a feed. Material’s phone rule that belongs here: do not show a navigation bar and a toolbar at the same time, because both occupy the bottom. Use the bar on primary pages and a toolbar on a later page that has actions ([Toolbar guidelines](https://m3.material.io/components/bottom-app-bar/guidelines)). Send above the keyboard is that later-page pattern. Atoms’ documented bar is not the point; the point is a cap of three, with Shop-like extras refused. Neither Blue Bottle post gives a conversion number. The Built In Q&A was edited for length.

6. **Screen rhythm.** The implied rhythm is place, then order, then wait. Only the wait is described as a different kind of screen. Order and location layouts were not opened. The Material example’s rhythm is a familiar email screen whose one change is the size and place of Send. A scattered album-art playlist, in the same research, looked new and failed because it destroyed the list. Atoms’ creation screen asks for the habit and the identity reason; the tracking example on the page is a large repetition count. That is undated.

7. **Risks.** The editorial slot can become a second app if it stops being about the order in progress. Style tokens were not published, so a team will invent them; inventing a second accent or a glass wait would break the refusal they did publish. Copying Material’s 35 shapes, asymmetric corners, and bouncy springs onto a three-item coffee app is the clutter risk. Google’s own research says a strong minority preferred calmer versions, that removing text labels hurt, and that what works in email or a media player might not suit a banking interface. A CHI 2026 abstract reports 33 percent faster time-to-first-fixation (1.60s versus 2.40s) and 20 percent faster task completion, n=48, across 10 apps. Google’s essay says people spotted key elements up to four times faster. Those numbers are not the same statistic; the paper body was not opened ([CHI 2026 abstract](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790373)). Reduced-motion behavior for Material springs was not quoted from material.io. Apple’s rule, if you use springs at all, is to tighten them when Reduce Motion is on ([Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)).

8. **Scores.** Originality 4, readability 4, consistency 4, buildability 4. Three targets and one wait are shippable and were designed against illegible packaging, but the visual tokens were never published and the Material cousin is easy to over-decorate.

### The product page is two labeled verbs

1. **Name.** The product page is two labeled verbs.

2. **References.** StockX help, current in this research: Buy Now at the lowest Ask, or Make an Offer ([How to buy](https://stockx.com/help/articles/how-do-i-buy-on-stockx), [Offer versus Buy Now](https://stockx.com/help/articles/What-is-the-difference-between-Make-Offer-and-Buy-Now-when-purchasing), [About buying](https://stockx.com/about/buying/)). GOAT, via a ScreensDesign teardown: instant buy and a lower offer on the same product page. The capture year is unknown, and the page is not GOAT’s ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/goat-sneakers-apparel)).

3. **Layout.** The page is a market, not a buy card. StockX keeps anonymous bid and ask matching. The shopper Buys Now or Makes an Offer. What changes is the number, and which marketplace the item is in: Verified, Listings (also on the US iOS app), or Live (US iOS only). Some Live items start at $1, according to the about page. The Live screen itself was not opened. StockX Pro’s graph and spread, dated 29 September 2025, is a seller tool, not the consumer phone layout. GOAT keeps product photography and a sell action on the page. The shopper buys at the listed price or switches into an offer, which adds Top Offer and Last Sale. A circular control sets how long the offer lasts. Inspiration is a separate tab that recomposes the same goods as shoppable styled photos. Wants and Owns are lists of the same products.

4. **Style.** Not specified in the sources for StockX: palette, type, radius, stroke, or icon weight. GOAT, in the teardown only, is “minimalist black-and-white” with photography in front. No hex, type, radius, or icon weight was stated. The system refuses a single unlabeled price. Both numbers have to say what they are.

5. **Navigation and density.** Consumer density is the pair of actions plus enough market context to tell them apart, not a long spec sheet. GOAT’s named surfaces are product, Wants, Owns, Inspiration, and Sell. A full tab list was not in the teardown. Offer UI adds data rather than more navigation. Safe areas and bar heights are not specified in the sources.

6. **Screen rhythm.** Home is search into a product. The product page is the market. A secondary mode is another marketplace or, on GOAT, Inspiration, still in the same black-and-white system the teardown describes. Bag and checkout are where GOAT’s recording found friction: Add to Cart can be disabled because the price “fluctuates rapidly,” and the path is forced through Checkout. Account and order status were not described for StockX.

7. **Risks.** Two prices look broken if they are not labeled in the same units. Older help still says Bid where current help says Make an Offer, so the words themselves drift. Regional all-in pricing means a buyer’s lowest Ask can differ from a sale printed in history, because duties sit in the Ask; a chart of last sale next to your price can look inconsistent when the market is working ([Why a sale can exceed an Ask](https://stockx.com/help/articles/why-did-an-item-sell-for-higher-than-my-asking-price)). GOAT’s disabled cart is a comprehension failure, not a skin failure. The circular duration control is an unusual target; its size was not given. GOAT’s year is unknown, so this half of the direction is thinner than StockX’s help. No contrast ratio was published.

8. **Scores.** Originality 4, readability 3, consistency 4, buildability 4. The two verbs are a clear, buildable page, and they fail as soon as one price hides the cart or the labels disagree.

### Words pinned under the picture, queue at the bottom

1. **Name.** Words pinned under the picture, queue at the bottom.

2. **References.** Spotify’s own newsroom, 7 May 2025 and 4 February 2026 ([May 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-05-07/experience-a-new-dimension-of-music-discovery-with-more-controls-and-enhanced-tools/), [February 2026](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-02-04/lyric-translations-offline-previews/)). A second app with this stack was not confirmed. Apple Music’s fullscreen albums and tinted track lists are trade press only; no Apple page was opened, so they are not used as a pair. The Verge’s 22 May 2024 note on the Spotify Mix typeface is secondary: the press release was not opened ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2024/5/22/24162464/spotify-mix-typeface-font-announcement-design-update)).

3. **Layout.** Album art or a short Canvas clip stays at the top of Now Playing. From February 2026, lyric previews sit directly under that art, on iPhone, Android, and tablet, Free and Premium. Translations, if present, sit under the original line. The three-dot menu still turns lyrics off. The queue stays the bottom control of that view, as redesigned in May 2025, even after the words move up. What may change is whether the words are there, and whether the queue shows tracks Spotify will add after yours. Library stays a bottom destination, not a second now-playing. Home shelves were not described.

4. **Style.** Not specified in the sources: palette, radius, stroke, or icon weight. The lyric line is the style rule that was stated: words belong to the art, not to a separate card somewhere else. A translate icon sits on the lyrics card. Spotify Mix is reported as the in-app face from May 2024, with sound-wave shapes in the letterforms. That is identity, not a layout, and it is unverified against Spotify’s own release. Canvas is motion under the words. 5. **Navigation and density.** Now Playing is a tall stack: art, words, controls, queue. 6. **Screen rhythm.** Now Playing is the detail. The rest of the app, in these two posts, is Library as a tab and playlist tools at the top of a list. Home was not opened. Turning lyrics off returns the stage to art and queue without a second visual language.

7. **Risks.** The stack becomes a dashboard. A translation is a second line on a player that already grew in 2025. Canvas moves behind the words. Spotify says early tests made lyrics easier to engage with and helped return plays, and also ships an off switch, so the line is not considered required. No type size or contrast figure was given. Offline lyrics are Premium-only, which is a content limit, not a layout. Do not add a glass ground sampled from the artwork; that idea is an unverified Apple Music report and a known contrast risk.

8. **Scores.** Originality 4, readability 3, consistency 3, buildability 3. Pinning one line under the art is a real structural move, and the documented queue already holds enough extra controls to crowd it.

### The reading page may drop its action bar

1. **Name.** The reading page may drop its action bar.

## 4. The theme to lock

**Name.** Paper column.

**Assumption.** This is for a consumer or premium product that has a primary object — a story, a product, a place, or a task — plus one utility mode. It is a poor fit for a market with two live prices, and a poor fit for a map-first product. Use those directions instead of forcing them into this frame.

The theme takes The Atlantic’s structure and Apple News Format’s licensed bleed. It is not a clone of The Atlantic. It does not take the games rainbow, the mini player over the mode bar, the clipped chips, or a translucent bar. It does not take Liquid Glass, and it does not take the local catalog’s gold accent. Tokens the sources do not give are marked, not invented.

### Layout rules

- The screen is a masthead, or a short title in that same place, then one column. The column is inset. On a phone, do not draw the News Format’s seven columns. That grid (default 7 columns, designed width 1024 pt, margin 60, gutter 20, from the indexed docs) is only the license: one named component may bleed to the screen edge, or the body may overlap the lower edge of a header image. Rows, chips, captions, and lists may not bleed. If the header contains type, do not overlap that type. A side caption that would sit in a wide margin moves under the image on a narrow screen.
- The front shows one lead and the start of the next object. The next object’s title and its control stay fully on screen, above the bar. Do not fill the front with equal cards, carousels that were not designed, or a stack of widgets.
- The mode bar has four sections, and only sections: Home, Latest, one short name for the single utility, and You. Search lives in the masthead, not as a fifth tab. You is the account. Do not also put a person icon that opens the same place. The bar does not hold create, bag, listen, or log. Letterboxd’s lesson applies if you truly have one primary verb: put that verb in the center of this bar only by replacing one of the four sections, not by adding a fifth item or a floating button.
- One bottom container. Section screens use the mode bar. The task screen removes it and shows one contextual action bar. Never both. This follows Material’s rule against a navigation bar and a toolbar together. It refuses the Atlantic author frame, where a mini player and the mode bar share the bottom. It also refuses a glass bar that shrinks on scroll as the brand idea. The 9 June 2025 shrink line is a search extract of the Newsroom URL, not a full article read, and the live tab-bar page ties minimizing to a bar that has an accessory ([Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). iPhone Duo may move the same bar to the side; do not hard-code “always the bottom edge,” and do not invent a second bar for that device.
- Type and controls stay in the safe area, clear of the status bar, the home indicator, and the bar. A primary control’s hit area is at least 44×44 pt, and at least 48×48 dp where the target is Android, with at least 8 px between targets. Do not use Apple’s 28×28 pt control floor, or an 11 pt caption, as the size of something people must tap or read.

### Style rules

- The ground is an opaque paper surface. Ink is near-black. One accent marks the active tab, a text link, and the primary action. Photography may be full color. The Atlantic frames use red as that accent; this system does not have to. No hex is specified in those frames, so do not invent one, and do not add a second accent. Do not put glass, blur, refraction, or a tinted photograph behind the words. Content stays on an opaque or standard surface. Apple forbids Liquid Glass in the content layer.
- Titles are a serif. Body is a plain text face at about 17 pt, the iOS default, with contrast of at least 4.5:1 for normal text. Apple’s checker uses 3:1 at 18 pt and for any bold text; a large title may use that lower bar, and body text may not. Do not set body under 16 px. Do not put a price or a caption at 11 px. The Atlantic frames do not name the serif. An unofficial May 2026 extraction of aesop.com, not a brand book and not a confirmed app, uses 14 px running text and 11 px prices. Refuse both. Text reflows for Dynamic Type, including stacking a side image under the title when type grows. Do not scale the tab labels just because the body grows. Hierarchy stays: the primary thing toward the top.
- Reading screens use no card shadow and no box around the lead. A hairline in the ink color may separate a section. The utility screen may round its panels. Do not mix square reading type and rounded utility panels on the same screen. Numeric radii are not specified in the Atlantic frames. Do not import Material’s 20, 32, and 48 dp steps, or its 35 shapes, as decoration.
- Icons are one weight, drawn simply, with no emoji. In the mode bar, use a filled symbol, which is Apple’s tab-bar guidance, and keep that weight everywhere else. Symbols do not get their own circular badges. The bar, or the action bar, is the container. Do not tint a label so it disappears into a bright photo.
- Motion is a fade, or a move that follows the finger. When reduced motion is on, tighten springs, do not bounce, and do not animate through a blur. Do not use a page-flip, a specular highlight, or a parallax that leaves type on a photograph without a legibility treatment. One hero moment on the front is enough. A second animated layer is clutter.

### Six screens

Each screen changes one thing. The masthead position, the column, the single accent, and the rule of one bottom container do not change.

1. **Front.** Layout change: the lead image is the one full-bleed, under the masthead. One headline block sits with it. Only the top of the next object shows, and that top stays above the four-item bar.
2. **Latest.** Layout change: the bleed is withdrawn. The column is titles with a small image, one object per row. Same masthead, same bar.
3. **Object.** Layout change: the header image may bleed, and the first lines may overlap only the empty lower edge of that image. After that, the body is an inset column. The mode bar stays. Nothing floats on the picture.
4. **Maker.** Layout change: the wordmark is replaced by a back control and a centered name, then a hairline, then the same column as Latest. The portrait is not covered. The mode bar stays.
5. **Utility.** Layout change: the prose column is replaced by a short stack of panels that use the paper ground and the one accent, not a new set of hues. The same four-item bar stays. The last chip or row sits fully inside the panel and fully above the bar.
6. **Task.** Layout change: the mode bar leaves, and one action bar takes the bottom, with one primary control and at most one secondary. Leaving the task brings the mode bar back. There is no moment with both.

Rename the utility tab for the product (Guide, Saved, Games) only if that word is the one utility. Do not add Audio as a fifth section. Listening, reserving, or continuing is the Task screen.

### Anti-patterns

- Liquid Glass, blur, or any translucent material on the content, including a bar whose look depends on default translucency. Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency will change it. A gold accent paired with that glass in a style catalog is not this theme.
- A second accent, a games rainbow (pink, orange, and yellow together), neon, a purple-blue gradient, or a progress line that introduces a new hue family on a reading screen.
- A mini player, a floating action button, or a toolbar stacked on the mode bar. Also a shrinking glass tab bar used as the brand.
- More than four tabs, a More tab, or an action (create, buy, listen, log) stored in the tab bar.
- Body text under 16 px, captions or prices at 11 px, light type weights at small sizes, and emoji as icons.
- Equal card stacks, bento tiles, a widget dashboard, edge-to-edge chips that run off a panel, and a lead so tall that the next object is only a clipped headline.
- Thirty-five decorative shapes, asymmetric corners with no job, and springs that bounce on a utilitarian screen.
- A two-price market, a map canvas, or a swipe-video home forced into this column. The February 2026 Shop swipe is a secondary report of a LinkedIn post that was not opened. It is not a reference for this theme.
- Copying packaging, a poster trend, or an unofficial website extraction (Aesop’s May 2026 page tokens) as if it were the app.
